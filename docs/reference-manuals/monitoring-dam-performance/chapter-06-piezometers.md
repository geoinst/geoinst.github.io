# Chapter 6 — Piezometers and Pore-Pressure Monitoring

## 6.1 Why pore pressure is central

Pore pressure — the pressure of water within soil, rock, or concrete — drives
seepage, slope stability, and uplift. It is among the most important quantities
monitored on dams. A rise in pore pressure can precede both internal erosion and
slope failure.

## 6.2 Types of piezometers

| Type | Principle | Best for |
|------|----------|---------|
| **Vibrating-wire (VW)** | Frequency change of a tensioned wire | Long-term automated monitoring; robust |
| **Pneumatic** | Gas pressure balances water pressure | Remote/ungaged locations, no power |
| **Standpipe (open)** | Water level in a pipe | Simple, direct, but slow to equalize |
| **Hydraulic (twin-tube)** | Liquid-column balance | Multiple points, established method |

!!! warning "Saturation is everything"
    An unsaturated VW piezometer reads nonsense. The **saturation procedure** —
    removing air from the porous tip and connecting tube — must be done carefully
    at installation. A piezometer that was never properly saturated is a silent gap
    in the program. (See [Dunnicliff Ch. 9](../dunnicliff/index.md).)

## 6.3 Where to place them

Piezometer location follows the failure modes:

- **Downstream of cores and cutoffs** to detect seepage buildup.
- **In the foundation** beneath and downstream of the dam.
- **At the phreatic surface** to track the seepage line through the embankment.
- **At the base of concrete dams** to monitor uplift.

Multiple depths at a single borehole (multi-level or MPBX-style) reveal the
vertical pore-pressure profile.

## 6.4 Installation essentials

- Drill and case the borehole; avoid smearing the surrounding soil.
- Place the tip in the stratum of interest with proper filter packs.
- Backfill and seal to prevent short-circuiting of water along the borehole.
- Saturate VW and pneumatic tips fully.
- Record installation details — depth, stratum, orientation, date — in the permanent file.

## 6.5 Reading and interpreting

Readings are typically converted to **piezometric head** (elevation of the
equivalent water surface). Key interpretations:

- **Steady rise without a reservoir rise** → possible blockage, seepage increase, or sensor fault.
- **Pore pressure lag** relative to reservoir → indicates drainage path and permeability.
- **Exceedance of the phreatic line used in design** → stability concern.

## 6.6 Common problems

- **Air entry / desaturation** → drifting or unresponsive readings.
- **Borehole short-circuit** → reads the wrong zone.
- **Freeze damage** → lost winter data in cold climates.
- **Electrical/cable failure** → most common automated-instrument failure.

## 6.7 Key takeaways

- Pore pressure drives seepage, slope stability, and uplift — monitor it carefully.
- Saturate VW and pneumatic tips properly; an unsaturated piezometer is useless.
- Place instruments where failure modes manifest; use multi-level installations.
- Interpret as piezometric head and compare against the design phreatic surface.
