# Chapter 8 — Deformation Monitoring: Survey and Geotechnical Sensors

## 8.1 Movement is the ultimate signal

Deformation — movement of the dam or foundation — is often the most direct sign of
distress. It is monitored both at the surface (survey) and internally
(geotechnical sensors).

## 8.2 Surface deformation — survey

A **survey control network** of monuments on the dam crest, slopes, and abutments
is the backbone of deformation monitoring:

- **Total station / GNSS** measures horizontal and vertical position to millimeters.
- **Frequency** ranges from monthly in steady state to daily during construction
  or after an event.
- **Datums** must be on stable ground outside the zone of influence.

Survey reveals settlement, crest shortening, and slope bulging.

## 8.3 Internal deformation — inclinometers

An **inclinometer** measures lateral displacement of a casing installed in a
borehole. It detects shear surfaces (the depth where movement concentrates) — the
signature of slope instability or internal erosion.

- Install casing through the zone of concern, anchored below it.
- Read from the bottom up; the bottom is the reference.
- **Casing integrity** is critical: a cracked or offset casing gives false readings.

## 8.4 Internal deformation — extensometers and settlement systems

- **Extensometers** (rod, magnetic, wireline) measure movement between an anchor
  deep in stable ground and a point nearer the surface — detecting settlement or
  heave at specific depths.
- **Settlement systems** (liquid, magnetic, settlement gauges) track vertical
  movement across an embankment.

## 8.5 Tiltmeters and crackmeters

- **Tiltmeters** sense rotation of a surface; useful on concrete structures and
  steep slopes.
- **Crackmeters / joint meters** measure opening of cracks or joints in concrete
  dams, abutments, and foundations.

## 8.6 Combining measurements

Deformation is most powerful when correlated: a surface survey shows *where* the
dam moved; an inclinometer shows *at what depth*; piezometers show *why*
(pore-pressure change). Together they localize the mechanism.

!!! tip "Build a deformation baseline early"
    Embankments settle as they consolidate; the first years define the normal
    settlement rate. Later acceleration stands out clearly against that baseline.

## 8.7 Key takeaways

- Deformation is the most direct distress signal; monitor it inside and out.
- Survey networks give surface movement; inclinometers reveal shear depth.
- Extensometers and settlement systems give depth-resolved vertical movement.
- Correlate deformation with piezometers to identify mechanism and location.
