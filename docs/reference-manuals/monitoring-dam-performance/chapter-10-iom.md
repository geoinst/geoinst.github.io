# Chapter 10 — Installation, Operation, and Maintenance

## 10.1 Reliability is made, not bought

The value of a monitoring program is the data it actually delivers over decades.
Most data loss comes not from the sensor but from poor installation, neglected
maintenance, and lost institutional knowledge. This chapter is about getting
reliable data for the life of the dam.

## 10.2 Installation

Good installation is disciplined fieldwork:

- **Protect the sensor** during drilling, grouting, and backfill.
- **Record everything** — depths, strata, orientations, cable routes, photos.
- **Verify at installation** — take initial readings and confirm the sensor responds.
- **Label and map** every instrument and cable.

A permanent **instrument record file** (IRF) documents each instrument from cradle
to grave.

## 10.3 Initial readings and zeroing

The first readings establish the baseline. Take them carefully, repeat them, and
store them with full metadata. Many "anomalies" are actually bad initial readings.

## 10.4 Operation

- Define and follow a **reading schedule** ([Chapter 4](chapter-04-planning.md)).
- Use calibrated readout equipment.
- Log who read what, when, and under what conditions.
- Cross-check automated vs. manual readings periodically.

## 10.5 Maintenance

Instruments degrade. A maintenance program includes:

- **Scheduled checks** (e.g., seasonal), cleaning, and battery replacement.
- **Spare parts** stocked for the specific models in use.
- **Calibration** at intervals appropriate to the technology.
- **Corrective action** when a sensor drifts, drops out, or fails.

!!! example "A dead instrument is a blind spot"
    If the single piezometer guarding a failure mode fails and is not repaired, the
    program has a hole exactly where it matters most. Track instrument *uptime* as a
    program metric.

## 10.6 Surviving staff and system changes

Programs outlive their creators. Protect against knowledge loss by:

- Keeping the monitoring plan and IRFs current.
- Training successors.
- Migrating data to systems that will still run in ten years.

## 10.7 Key takeaways

- Reliability is built in the field — protect, record, and verify at installation.
- Take and protect initial readings; they are the baseline.
- Maintain on a schedule with spares and calibration.
- Preserve the plan and instrument records so the program survives staff turnover.
