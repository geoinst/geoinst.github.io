# Handbook of Geotechnical Investigation and Design Tables (Burt G. Look, 2007)

!!! note "About this reference"
    This page is an **independent, original digest** of *Handbook of Geotechnical
    Investigation and Design Tables* by **Burt G. Look** (Taylor & Francis / Balkema,
    2007, ISBN 978-0-415-43038-8). The book is **copyright © 2007 Taylor & Francis
    Group** — *all rights reserved* — and is **not** reproduced here as text. The
    original 356-page PDF is linked below as the source reference for comparison, in
    keeping with the site's established treatment of its other copyrighted source
    manuals. The digest below is original summary text, not a reproduction of the
    source. A full Vietnamese translation of a 356-page handbook is a multi-session
    effort; it is being built incrementally by chapter and will be linked here when
    available.

*Handbook of Geotechnical Investigation and Design Tables* is a **data book** for the
practising geotechnical engineer and engineering geologist. Rather than a textbook,
it is a compilation of **tables, correlations, and rules of thumb** that turn field
and laboratory data into usable design parameters — explicitly written for
experienced professionals who already grasp the fundamentals and need the
cross-referenced *ranges* that bracket a prediction when different methods disagree.

The author's stated thesis is the **bracketing of results by cross-checking
methods**: because the ground cannot be over-simplified and different references
give different answers for similar data, the handbook supplies the *typical values
and correlations* so an engineer can proceed and then validate against the cited
literature. It deliberately does **not** replace soil-mechanics fundamentals, which
it expects the reader to source elsewhere.

## Why this handbook matters in this knowledge base

- It is the **practical bridge between investigation and design** that the rest of
  this library theorises. [Physical Geology](../physical-geology/index.md) and
  [Soil Mechanics](../soil-mechanics/index.md) supply the physics; this book
  supplies the *numbers* — the correlations (SPT → strength, CPT → soil type, RQD →
  rock bearing capacity, PI → modulus) that an engineer actually types into a
  spreadsheet.
- Its **Chapter 23 (Movements)**, including **movements in inclinometers**, is the
  quantitative companion to the [Dunnicliff](../dunnicliff/index.md) and
  [FHWA](../fhwa/index.md) instrumentation philosophies and the tolerable-movement
  logic used in the [Mining](../../applications/mining/index.md) case studies.
- Its **Chapter 4 (field sampling & testing)** and **Chapters 5–6 (strength from
  testing)** make explicit why *uncorrected* SPT or CPT values mislead design —
  the same caution the [SME Mining Engineering Handbook (Ch. 8.5)](../sme-mine-handbook/index.md)
  raises about construction pore pressure.
- Its **risk, reliability and variability** chapters (10) formalise the probabilistic
  framing behind the site's [ADAQS](../../vi/adaqs/index.md) trigger levels.

## How to use this digest

- Treat it as an **index to the original** — open the PDF at the chapter/section you
  need; the table above tells you exactly where each correlation lives.
- Use the correlations to **bracket** a design parameter, then confirm against the
  cited references before relying on it for a final design or a regulatory
  submission — the author is explicit that these are guides, not a cook-book.
- Cross-link into [Foundation Engineering](../foundation-engineering/index.md),
  [Practical Rock Engineering](../practical-rock-engineering/index.md) and the
  [CFEM 2022 instrumentation chapter](../cfem/index.md) for the civil-side depth.
- Ask the [GTI Doctor](../../gti-doctor.md) to pull the exact table or correlation
  from the original PDF when you need a number.

> **Safety and currency.** The correlations reflect practice around 2007 and an
> Australian/UK field context. Treat the *method* (cross-check by method, bracket
> the answer) as durable and **verify current values** with modern local codes,
> the cited standards, and applicable regulation before using them for design or a
> regulatory submission.
