# SME Mining Engineering Handbook — Geotechnical Instrumentation (Chapter 8.5)

!!! note "About this reference"
    This page is an **original, open digest** of **Chapter 8.5 — *Geotechnical
    Instrumentation*** of the **SME Mining Engineering Handbook** (3rd edition),
    authored by **Erik Eberhardt** (University of British Columbia) and **Doug
    Stead** (Simon Fraser University). It is a chapter-level summary — **not** a
    reproduction of the copyrighted source text. The handbook is published by the
    Society for Mining, Metallurgy & Exploration (SME); the original chapter and its
    figures remain the copyright of the authors and the publisher. The digest is
    offered for education and orientation; a full Vietnamese translation is provided
    alongside it, and the original English PDF is reproduced here for reference.

**Chapter 8.5, *Geotechnical Instrumentation***, is one of the most-cited modern
surveys of instrumentation practice in mining. Written by two of the field's
leading rock-mechanics academics, it covers **21 pages** (pp. 551–570) and is
organised around a single organising idea: instrumentation serves **two distinct
functions** — the *investigative* function (collecting the input parameters a
design model needs) and the *monitoring* function (observing how the ground
actually responds, and warning when it departs from design).

That framing is what makes the chapter useful. Rather than an instrument
catalogue, it reads as a **decision sequence**: what question are you trying to
answer, what parameter answers it, what instrument measures that parameter with
sufficient range, resolution, accuracy and reliability — and how do you get the
data back in a form somebody can act on?

## Scope of this reference

| # | Section | What it covers |
|---|---------|----------------|
| 1 | **Introduction** | Investigative vs. monitoring functions; the seven instrument-selection criteria — range, resolution, accuracy, precision, conformance, robustness, reliability |
| 2 | **Instrumentation for investigation** | Borehole techniques (core orientation, televiewers, borehole geophysics); remote sensing (LiDAR, digital photogrammetry); surface geophysics; groundwater characterisation (piezometers, flow testing); in-situ stress measurement (overcoring, hydraulic fracturing) |
| 3 | **Instrumentation for monitoring** | Dunnicliff's 20-step planning approach; transducer types; surface displacement (geodetic/RTS, GPS); extensometers; tiltmeters; borehole displacement (probe and in-place inclinometers, borehole extensometers, convergence systems, shaped accelerometer arrays, TDR, fibre optics); remote sensing of deformation (InSAR, slope radar, LiDAR/photogrammetry); pressure cells; microseismicity |
| 4 | **Data acquisition and presentation** | Wireless data transmission; data immersion and visualisation; integrated mine-data models |
| 5 | **References** | A 64-entry reference list spanning ISRM suggested methods, SAIMM slope-stability symposia, and the primary journal literature |

The chapter is illustrated with **14 figures** and **5 tables**, all of which are
reproduced in the Vietnamese translation below.

## Why this chapter matters in this knowledge base

Chapter 8.5 is the **mining-side counterpart** to the civil-side material the rest
of this library is built on:

- Its **selection-criteria framework** (range, resolution, accuracy, precision,
  conformance, robustness, reliability) is the vocabulary this site uses whenever
  an instrument is specified — see [Getting Started](../../getting-started/index.md).
- Its **transducer table** (LVDT, vibrating wire, accelerometer, fibre optic, MEMS)
  explains *why* each instrument behaves the way it does, and is the natural
  companion to the [Dunnicliff](../dunnicliff/index.md) and
  [FHWA](../fhwa/index.md) digests.
- Its **Dunnicliff 20-step planning procedure** is the organising spine behind the
  site's vendor-neutral [ADAQS six-layer architecture](../../vi/adaqs/index.md).
- Its **in-situ stress** and **piezometer** sections give the rock-mechanics and
  groundwater grounding that [Soil Mechanics](../soil-mechanics/index.md) and
  [Foundation Engineering](../foundation-engineering/index.md) approach from the
  soil side.

## How to use this reference

- **Read the selection criteria first** (§1). Most instrumentation failures are
  specification failures, not sensor failures.
- Use the **Dunnicliff 20-step table** as a checklist before you buy anything —
  steps 1–8 are the ones most often skipped.
- Work through **investigation before monitoring**: the chapter is explicit that
  predictive monitoring should follow a period of investigative monitoring.
- Cross-link into [Dunnicliff](../dunnicliff/index.md),
  [FHWA](../fhwa/index.md), [Monitoring Dam Performance](../monitoring-dam-performance/index.md)
  and [Tailings Dam Safety](../tailings-dam-safety/index.md) for civil-side depth.
- Ask the [GTI Doctor](../../gti-doctor.md) to pull specifics from the wider corpus.

> **Safety and currency.** Remote-sensing and wireless-sensor technology has moved
> considerably since this chapter was written — InSAR revisit times, radar
> precision and fibre-optic distributed sensing all continue to improve. Treat the
> **principles and selection logic** as durable and **verify current instrument
> specifications** with manufacturers before relying on them for a design or
> regulatory submission.
