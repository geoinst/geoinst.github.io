# Chapter 6 — Seepage & Flow Nets

## 6.1 Seepage and its consequences

**Seepage** is the flow of water through soil under a hydraulic gradient. It matters
because it:

- exerts **seepage forces** on the soil skeleton, reducing stability;
- causes **uplift** pressures on structures;
- can trigger **piping** and **internal erosion**, the mechanism of many dam and
  levee failures; and
- drives **consolidation** and the rate of settlement.

## 6.2 Flow nets

A **flow net** is a graphical solution of Laplace's equation for steady seepage. It
consists of two orthogonal families of curves:

- **Flow lines** — the paths along which water travels; and
- **Equipotential lines** — lines of equal total head.

The net is drawn so that the elements are **"square"**. From it, the engineer reads:

- the **quantity of seepage** $q = k H (N_f / N_d)$;
- the **pore pressure** (and thus uplift) at any point;
- the **exit gradient** at the downstream surface, which governs piping.

## 6.3 Uplift and buoyancy

Water pressure acts **upward** on a structure or soil mass, reducing effective
stress. Under a dam or floor slab, the **uplift** pressure is read from the flow net
and must be resisted by the structure's weight. Where uplift reduces effective
stress to zero, the soil **floats** and loses all strength.

## 6.4 Piping and internal erosion

**Piping** occurs when the **exit gradient** at the downstream surface exceeds a
critical value, so that water lifts and carries away soil particles, forming a
pipe that progresses backward and can cause collapse. The **factor of safety against
piping** compares the critical gradient (often ~1.0, or lower for safety) with the
actual exit gradient.

Mitigation includes:

- **longer seepage paths** (cut-off walls, blankets);
- **filters and drains** that intercept and safely discharge seepage; and
- **relief wells** to control uplift.

## 6.5 Filters and drainage

**Filters** protect against internal erosion by allowing water to pass while
retaining soil particles. Design follows **filter criteria** (the filter must be
coarse enough to be permeable and fine enough to retain the protected soil).
**Drainage layers** and **geotextiles** perform the same function.

## 6.6 Key takeaways

- **Seepage forces** reduce stability and cause **uplift**.
- A **flow net** gives seepage quantity, pore pressure, and **exit gradient**.
- **Piping** occurs when the exit gradient is too high — check a factor of safety.
- **Filters and drains** are the standard protection against internal erosion.
- Seepage drives **consolidation** and must be managed in design.
