# Chapter 7 — Effective Stress & Pore Pressure

## 7.1 The principle of effective stress

**Terzaghi's principle** is the foundation of soil mechanics:

$$\sigma' = \sigma - u$$

where $\sigma$ is the **total stress** at a point, $u$ is the **pore-water
pressure**, and $\sigma'$ is the **effective stress** carried by the solid skeleton.
**Every aspect of soil behaviour — strength, stiffness, and volume change — depends
on effective stress, not total stress.**

## 7.2 Computing total stress

Total stress at depth is the sum of the weights of everything above:

$$\sigma_v = \sum \gamma_i h_i$$

using the appropriate unit weight for each layer — **bulk** above the water table,
**saturated** below it.

## 7.3 Pore pressure

Pore pressure is measured with **piezometers** (see the
[Dunnicliff](../dunnicliff/index.md) and [FHWA](../fhwa/index.md) references).
Two states matter:

- **Hydrostatic (steady)** — $u = \gamma_w z$, increasing with depth below the water
  table.
- **Excess pore pressure** — generated by loading or unloading faster than the water
  can drain; it dissipates over time (consolidation).

## 7.4 Effective stress profiles

Combining the two gives the **effective stress profile**. Key observations:

- Below the water table, effective stress increases with the **submerged** unit
  weight, not the saturated one.
- An **upward seepage** gradient reduces effective stress; a **downward** gradient
  increases it.
- Where effective stress reaches **zero**, the soil loses all strength
  (liquefaction, heave, boiling).

## 7.5 Why it matters

Effective stress explains:

- **Consolidation** — settlement from dissipation of excess pore pressure
  ([Chapter 9](chapter-09-consolidation.md));
- **Shear strength** — governed by $\sigma'$ ([Chapter 10](chapter-10-shear-strength.md));
- **Liquefaction** — loss of effective stress under cyclic loading; and
- **Seepage stability** — uplift and piping ([Chapter 6](chapter-06-seepage.md)).

## 7.6 Key takeaways

- **$\sigma' = \sigma - u$** — the central principle of soil mechanics.
- Total stress is the **sum of layer weights**; pore pressure comes from
  **piezometers**.
- Below the water table use **submerged** unit weight for effective stress.
- **Seepage changes** effective stress — upward flow reduces it.
- Effective stress drives **consolidation, strength, and liquefaction**.
