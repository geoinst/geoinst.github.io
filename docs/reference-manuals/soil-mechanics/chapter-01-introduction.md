# Chapter 1 — Introduction and Sources

## 1.1 What soil mechanics is

**Soil** is a particulate, three-phase material — **solid particles, water, and
air** — whose behaviour depends on the interaction between the phases. **Soil
mechanics** studies how that material responds to stress, water, and time. It
underpins every geotechnical calculation: bearing capacity, settlement, slope
stability, earth pressure, and seepage.

## 1.2 The three phases and why they matter

Almost every soil property is explained by the three phases:

- The **solid skeleton** carries the effective stress and gives strength and
  stiffness.
- **Water** in the pores carries pore pressure and governs consolidation and
  seepage.
- **Air** makes the soil unsaturated, adding suction and apparent cohesion.

The relationship between the phases — expressed by **void ratio**, **water
content**, and **degree of saturation** — is the starting point of every analysis.

## 1.3 Why this reference uses public-domain sources

The classic soil-mechanics textbooks are **copyrighted**. This reference is instead
built from the **FHWA NHI *Soils and Foundations Reference Manual*** and the
**USACE Engineering Manuals** — **U.S. Government works in the public domain**,
free to reuse, adapt, and translate. They are written for practising engineers and
form the basis of real design in the United States.

## 1.4 Total and effective stress — the central idea

The single most important concept in soil mechanics is **Terzaghi's principle of
effective stress**:

$$\sigma' = \sigma - u$$

where $\sigma$ is total stress, $u$ is pore-water pressure, and $\sigma'$ is
**effective stress** — the stress carried by the solid skeleton. **Strength,
stiffness, and volume change depend on effective stress, not total stress.** This
idea threads through every later chapter.

## 1.5 Key takeaways

- Soil is a **three-phase** material; its behaviour follows from the phases.
- **Effective stress $\sigma' = \sigma - u$** governs strength, stiffness, and
  volume change.
- This book is built **entirely from public-domain FHWA and USACE manuals**.
- Soil parameters carry **uncertainty** — good engineering bounds it.
