# Chapter 3 — Soil Classification

## 3.1 Why classify

Classification groups soils with similar engineering behaviour into a few named
categories. It lets an engineer communicate, correlate with local experience, and
make first estimates of strength, compressibility, and drainage — before any
testing. It is a **language**, not a design method.

## 3.2 The Unified Soil Classification System (USCS)

The **USCS** is the standard in geotechnical practice. It uses:

- the **grain-size distribution** (percent passing the No. 200 sieve) to separate
  **coarse-grained** from **fine-grained** soils;
- the **$C_u$ and $C_c$** to distinguish **well-graded (W)** from **poorly-graded
  (P)** coarse soils; and
- the **Atterberg limits** (via the plasticity chart) to classify fines as **silt
  (M)**, **clay (C)**, or **organic (O)** of **low (L)** or **high (H)**
  plasticity.

Major groups: **GW, GP, GM, GC** (gravels); **SW, SP, SM, SC** (sands); **ML, CL,
OL, MH, CH, OH** (silts, clays, organics); and **Pt** (peat).

## 3.3 The AASHTO system

The **AASHTO** system (used in highway engineering) classifies soils into groups
**A-1 through A-7** for use in pavement and embankment design, with a **group index**
reflecting quality. A-1 and A-2 are granular and good; A-6 and A-7 are clayey and
poor.

## 3.4 What classification does not tell you

Classification is based on **index properties**, so it **cannot** give:

- the actual **strength** or **stiffness** (two CLs can differ widely);
- the **density state** (loose vs. dense sand both classify as SP);
- the **in-situ structure** (cementation, fabric, fissures);
- the **stratigraphy** — one sample does not describe a site.

Classification must always be confirmed by **testing and observation**.

## 3.5 Key takeaways

- Classification is a **language** for grouping soils by behaviour.
- **USCS** uses grain size + Atterberg limits; **AASHTO** uses groups A-1 to A-7.
- It gives **first estimates** but not actual strength, density, or structure.
- Always confirm with **testing and field observation**.
