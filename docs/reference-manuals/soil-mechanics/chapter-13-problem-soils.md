# Chapter 13 — Problem Soils

## 13.1 What makes a soil "problematic"

Some soils behave in ways that surprise engineers who treat them as ordinary
material. They are **problem soils** because their behaviour is governed by
properties — volume change, collapse, organic content, or liquefaction potential —
that standard classification does not capture.

## 13.2 Expansive (swelling) clays

Expansive clays **swell** when wetted and **shrink** when dried, causing heave and
cracking of foundations, pavements, and structures. They are identified by:

- high **plasticity index** and **shrinkage limit**;
- **free-swell** and **swell-pressure** tests; and
- mineralogy (montmorillonite).

Mitigation: **moisture control**, **foundation depth below the active zone**,
**replacement** with non-expansive fill, **chemical stabilization** (lime), and
**membranes** to limit moisture change.

## 13.3 Collapsible soils

Collapsible soils — loose silts, loess, and some fills — **collapse suddenly** when
wetted under load, because their open structure is held by weak bonds. They are
identified by **double oedometer tests** (comparing natural and saturated
compression) and by **in-situ collapse potential** tests.

Mitigation: **pre-wetting**, **dynamic compaction**, **grouting**, **deep
foundations** to bypass the collapsible layer, and **moisture control**.

## 13.4 Organic soils and peat

Organic soils and **peat** are highly compressible, weak, and prone to **large
primary and secondary** settlement. They have:

- high **water content** and **void ratio**;
- high **compressibility** and **secondary compression**;
- low **strength** and **stiffness**.

Mitigation: **excavation and replacement**, **preloading** with or without
**vertical drains**, **deep foundations**, and **lightweight fill**.

## 13.5 Liquefiable soils

**Liquefaction** is the loss of strength in loose, saturated sands under cyclic
loading (earthquake or vibration), when pore pressure rises to equal total stress.
It is assessed by comparing the **cyclic stress ratio** with the **cyclic resistance
ratio** from SPT/CPT.

Mitigation: **densification** (vibro, dynamic compaction), **drainage** (stone
columns, drains), **cementation** (grouting), and **reinforcement** (piles,
micropiles).

## 13.6 Other problem soils

- **Sensitive clays** — lose strength dramatically when remoulded.
- **Dispersive clays** — erode readily in water; a piping risk.
- **Fills** — highly variable; must be characterised, not assumed.
- **Frozen ground** — heave and thaw settlement.

## 13.7 Key takeaways

- **Problem soils** behave outside standard classification.
- **Expansive clays** heave/shrink — control moisture and depth.
- **Collapsible soils** collapse on wetting — pre-wet, densify, or bypass.
- **Organic soils** settle greatly — excavate, preload, or use deep foundations.
- **Liquefiable sands** lose strength under cyclic loading — densify or drain.
