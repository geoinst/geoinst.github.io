# Transportation Geotechnics

!!! abstract "Session Summary"
    Session 10 of *GeoVadis (GAIC 2025)* investigates the demanding geotechnical challenges of highway and railway infrastructure. Core themes encompass back-to-back Mechanically Stabilized Earth (MSE) walls under cyclic train loading, recycled rubber grids from waste conveyor belts for ballast degradation control, 3D dynamic train-track-subgrade modeling, geocell-stabilized expansive pavement subgrades, EPS geofoam lightweight highway embankments, and pavement structural responses to electric vehicle (EV) axle loading.

---

## 1. Back-to-Back MSE Walls Under Heavy Rail Loading (S. Attara, B. Umashankar)

### Constrained Approach Embankments
In narrow transportation corridors and bridge approach ramps, retaining walls are constructed back-to-back with overlapping or connected geogrid reinforcement layers. Attara and Umashankar analyze the dynamic performance of back-to-back MSE walls subjected to cyclic train loading ($P_{axle} = 250\,\text{kN}$):
- **Overlapping vs. Connected Reinforcement:** When the distance between wall faces ($W$) is narrow ($W/H < 1.4$), connecting opposing geogrid layers directly prevents outward wall face bulging and reduces maximum lateral wall displacement by over $40\%$ compared to unconnected overlapping sheets.
- **Dynamic Lateral Earth Pressure:** High-speed wheel passage generates transient dynamic earth pressure spikes that attenuate exponentially with depth. Maximum geogrid tensile strains concentrate within the upper $0.3H$ zone immediately below the track slab.

## 2. Waste Conveyor Belt Rubber Grids in Ballasted Tracks (S. Hettiyahandi, B. Indraratna, T. Ngo, Y. Qi, et al.)

### Circular Economy in High-Speed Rail Track Maintenance
Ballast breakage and settlement account for over $50\%$ of track maintenance budgets worldwide. Professor Indraratna’s research group develops perforated rubber grids upcycled from end-of-life industrial conveyor belts:
- **Energy Dissipation:** When placed beneath the ballast bed, the high damping ratio and resilience of the rubber grid absorb impact shock waves generated by wheel-rail irregularities.
- **Ballast Degradation Reduction:** Large-scale cyclic triaxial tests ($500,000$ cycles at $f = 15\,\text{Hz}$) demonstrate that rubber grids reduce the Ballast Breakage Index ($BBI$) by $45\% - 50\%$ compared to unreinforced ballast.
- **Track Resilient Modulus ($M_R$):** The composite layer dampens cyclic track acceleration while preserving track elasticity, significantly reducing ballast tamping frequencies.

## 3. Geocell Stabilization of Expansive Pavement Subgrades (S. Srivastava, B. Umashankar, P.K. Mahopatra, et al.)

### Controlling Differential Swelling-Shrinkage Cracking
Pavements constructed over high-plasticity expansive black cotton soils suffer from severe seasonal differential heave, longitudinal cracking, and alligator fatigue. Srivastava et al. investigate 3D geocell confinement mattresses installed at the subgrade-base interface:
- **All-Around Cellular Confinement:** Geocell pockets confine granular aggregate, generating high localized hoop stresses that convert vertical wheel loads into wide horizontal stress distributions.
- **Traffic Benefit Ratio ($TBR$):** Cyclic plate load testing and finite element modeling show that geocell reinforcement increases the Traffic Benefit Ratio ($TBR$) by $2.5 - 3.8$, permitting a $30\%$ reduction in asphalt layer thickness while completely suppressing surface rutting over wet-dry seasons.

## 4. Lightweight EPS Geofoam Embankments on Ultra-Soft Clays (V.V. Butle, G.S. Parvathi, A. Mandal, V. Srinivasan)

### Zero-Consolidation Settlement Over Coastal Mud
Constructing conventional fill embankments ($18 - 20\,\text{kN/m}^3$) over deep coastal clays induces colossal consolidation settlement and global slope instability. Butle et al. evaluate ultra-lightweight Expanded Polystyrene (EPS) geofoam blocks ($\gamma \approx 0.15 - 0.25\,\text{kN/m}^3$):
- **Gravity Load Compensation:** Replacing $3\,\text{m}$ of earth fill with EPS geofoam reduces total overburden pressure by over $90\%$, eliminating primary and secondary consolidation settlement.
- **Dynamic Rail/Highway Performance:** FE simulations confirm that reinforced concrete load-distribution slabs atop EPS blocks bound cyclic compressive strains well within elastic limits ($\epsilon < 1\%$), ensuring long-term fatigue durability.

## 5. Pavement Structural Demands Under Electric Vehicles (P. Chaudhary, S. Saride)

### Impact of Heavy Battery Packs on Pavement Longevity
Electric vehicles (EVs) feature heavy battery packs that increase vehicle curb weight by $20\% - 35\%$ compared to equivalent internal combustion engine (ICE) vehicles:
- **Axle Load Spectra Shifts:** Chaudhary and Saride analyze pavement damage ratios using heavy vehicle simulator data and Kenlayer viscoelastic modeling.
- **Fatigue & Rutting Damage:** The heavier EV fleet increases bottom-up asphalt tensile strains ($\epsilon_t$) and subgrade compressive strains ($\epsilon_v$), accelerating subgrade rutting rates by $28\% - 42\%$.
- **Design Recommendations:** Standard design codes must adopt revised Equivalent Single Axle Load (ESAL) truck factors and specify polymer-modified binders or geosynthetic interlayers to preserve pavement service life.
