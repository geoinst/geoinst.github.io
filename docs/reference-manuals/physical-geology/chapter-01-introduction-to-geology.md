# Chapter 1 — Introduction to Geology

Geology is the study of the Earth — its materials, its structure, and the processes that
change it. For the engineer the value is direct: **every foundation, cut and tunnel is an
intervention in a geological system**, and the ground's history explains its present
properties.

## 1.1 What geology covers

- **Materials** — minerals, rocks, and the unconsolidated sediments derived from them.
- **Structure** — how rock bodies are layered, folded, fractured and faulted.
- **Processes** — the internal (tectonic, igneous, metamorphic) and external (weathering,
  erosion, deposition) processes that form and destroy rock.
- **Time** — geological time is long; the ground the engineer meets is the accumulated
  result of it.

## 1.2 Minerals and rocks — the essential distinction

A **mineral** is a naturally occurring substance with a specific chemical composition and
an ordered internal structure. A **rock** is a mixture of one or more minerals. Feldspar,
quartz, mica, calcite and halite are *minerals*; granite, basalt, sandstone, limestone and
schist are *rocks*.

## 1.3 The three rock families

- **Igneous** — solidified from magma or lava. *Intrusive* (granite) cools slowly at depth
  and is coarse-grained; *extrusive* (basalt) cools quickly at the surface and is
  fine-grained.
- **Sedimentary** — formed from deposited and then buried sediment. Classified as
  *clastic* (by grain size) or *chemical/organic*.
- **Metamorphic** — formed from existing rock under heat and pressure; develops foliation.

## Key points for the geotechnical engineer

- **Mineral ≠ rock.** A rock's behaviour depends on its mineral assemblage *and* its
  structure, not on its name alone.
- Rock names describe **genesis**, not engineering performance — two granites can behave
  quite differently.
- The rock cycle means **any rock can be the parent of the soil above it**.
