# Chapter 14 — Groundwater

Groundwater is stored in the voids of soil and rock. Two properties decide everything:
**how much space there is** (porosity) and **how well those spaces are connected**
(permeability).

## 14.1 Porosity

**Porosity** is the percentage of a material's volume that is void. Unconsolidated
sediments are generally more porous than their consolidated equivalents, and finer materials
(silt, clay) are *more* porous than coarser ones.

![Figure: geology-porosity.png](../../assets/figures/geology/porosity.png)

**Figure.** Typical porosity ranges for unconsolidated materials (red) and rocks (blue). Silt and clay are the most porous materials — yet the least permeable.

## 14.2 Permeability and hydraulic conductivity

**Permeability** describes how well the pores are connected — and therefore how easily water
flows. It is quantified as **hydraulic conductivity $K$**, in metres per second.

![Figure: geology-hydraulic-conductivity.png](../../assets/figures/geology/hydraulic-conductivity.png)

**Figure.** Hydraulic conductivity K (m/s) for unconsolidated materials (red) and rocks (blue) — a range of some twelve orders of magnitude. Clay is porous but nearly impermeable.
*© Steven Earle, CC BY 4.0 — Physical Geology, 2nd ed., Fig. 14.1.2.*

The counter-intuitive but essential point: **clay is porous but not permeable.** Almost all
of its water is held as *bound water* on the surfaces of the mineral grains and cannot flow.
This is why a clay layer is a barrier, not a drain.

## 14.3 Aquifers and confining layers

- **Aquifer** — a body of ground that transmits water usefully.
- **Aquitard / confining layer** — a body that does not.
- **Unconfined aquifer** — open to the surface; the water table is its upper boundary.
- **Confined aquifer** — capped by a lower-permeability layer; its water is under pressure,
  so a piezometer reads above the top of the aquifer.

![Figure: geology-aquifers-confining.png](../../assets/figures/geology/aquifers-confining.png)

**Figure.** A cross-section of aquifers and confining layers, with relative permeabilities given as K (m/s).
*© Steven Earle, CC BY 4.0 — Physical Geology, 2nd ed., Fig. 14.1.4.*

## 14.4 Groundwater extraction and quality

Pumping changes the water table and creates **drawdown**, which causes settlement — a
classic cause of damage to structures near wells and deep excavations. Groundwater quality
also matters: aggressive groundwater corrodes steel and attacks concrete.

## Key points for the geotechnical engineer

- **Porosity ≠ permeability.** Clay is the proof.
- **Hydraulic conductivity spans 12 orders of magnitude** — the single most important
  number in any seepage or drainage calculation.
- **Confined aquifers are under pressure**; the piezometric level is not the water table.
- **Pumping causes settlement** — drawdown is a geotechnical, not just a water-supply,
  issue.
- **Groundwater chemistry** drives corrosion and concrete attack.
