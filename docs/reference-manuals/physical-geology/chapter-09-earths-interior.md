# Chapter 9 — Earth's Interior and Seismology

Almost everything known about the deep Earth comes from **seismic waves**. The same physics
is the basis of every seismic method used in geotechnical investigation — so this chapter is
both background and methodology.

## 9.1 Body waves

Two types of wave travel through the Earth's interior:

- **P waves (primary)** — compression waves. Fastest; travel through solids, liquids and
  gases.
- **S waves (secondary)** — shear waves. Slower; **travel through solids only**.

## 9.2 Velocity and material properties

Wave velocity depends on **elastic stiffness and density**. Because stiffness generally
increases with depth, velocity increases with depth — but it drops sharply wherever the
material is weak, fractured, or partially molten. A local drop in velocity is an
**anomaly**, and mapping anomalies is what seismic surveys do.

## 9.3 The layered Earth

- **Crust** — to about 30–40 km under continents, 5–10 km under oceans.
- **Moho** — the crust–mantle boundary, detected by the abrupt jump in wave velocity.
- **Mantle** — to 2 890 km; strong and dense.
- **Core** — an outer liquid part (S waves cannot pass) and a solid inner core.

## Key points for the geotechnical engineer

- **S-wave velocity is the key dynamic soil parameter** used in seismic site response and
  liquefaction assessment.
- **Velocity contrast = a detectable boundary.** The sediment/rock contrast is what seismic
  refraction exploits to find depth to bedrock.
- **S waves do not pass through liquid**, which is why they can detect saturated, soft or
  liquefiable layers.
- The **Moho** is the deepest discontinuity — but the ones that matter are metres, not
  kilometres, below the foundation.
