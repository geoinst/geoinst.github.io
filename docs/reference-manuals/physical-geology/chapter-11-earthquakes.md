# Chapter 11 — Earthquakes

An earthquake is the rapid release of accumulated elastic strain along a fault, radiating
seismic waves. For the engineer, the questions are: **how big, how far, and how does the
ground respond?**

## 11.1 Earthquake basics

- **Focus** — where rupture begins, inside the Earth.
- **Epicentre** — the point on the surface directly above the focus.
- **Fault** — the fracture along which displacement occurs. An **active fault** is one that
  has moved in recent geological time and may move again.

## 11.2 Measuring earthquakes

- **Magnitude $M$** — a single number for the *size of the event*, from the amplitude of
  ground motion or the seismic moment.
- **Intensity $I$** — a *description of the effects at a place*; varies with distance,
  ground conditions and building type.

Magnitude is a property of the earthquake; intensity is a property of the site.

## 11.3 Earthquakes and plate tectonics

Most earthquakes occur at plate boundaries — subduction zones, mid-ocean ridges and
transform faults — but **intraplate earthquakes** occur too, and Vietnam's seismicity
(notably the northwest, along the Red River and Lai Chau–Dien Bien fault zones, and parts
of the Central Highlands) is of that type.

## 11.4 Consequences and secondary hazards

Beyond direct shaking, earthquakes trigger **landslides**, **liquefaction**, **ground
subsidence** and **tsunami**. Saturation followed by shaking is the classic compound
trigger.

## Key points for the geotechnical engineer

- **Magnitude ≠ intensity.** Design needs the *ground motion at the site*, not the
  magnitude of the event.
- **Site conditions dominate the response.** Soft soils amplify shaking; rock does not.
- **Liquefaction requires saturation** — assess it wherever loose saturated sand lies below
  the water table.
- **Rain + earthquake** is the most damaging landslide trigger combination.
- **Vietnam is not aseismic** — seismic design is required in the northwest and parts of the
  Central Highlands.
