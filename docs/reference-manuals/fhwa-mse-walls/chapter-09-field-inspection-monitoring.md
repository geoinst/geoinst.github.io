# Chapter 9 — Field Inspection & Performance Monitoring

## 9.1 Preconstruction reviews

Before construction, review the **plans, specifications, and submittals** against
the design intent: the reinforcement type and layout, the fill specification, the
facing details, and the drainage system. Confirm that any supplier design has
been approved and that the specified fill is actually available from the project
borrow — a very common source of trouble is a specified fill that no local
source can meet.

## 9.2 Prefabricated materials inspection

Inspect the **reinforcement** (type, strength, dimensions, coating, and freedom
from damage), the **facing units** (panels or blocks, dimensions, and
reinforcement connection points), the **connectors**, and the **drainage and
filter materials** against the specification. Damaged or out-of-specification
materials are rejected before they are built into the structure, where they
cannot be seen again.

## 9.3 Construction control

The manual sets out the control points for each construction stage:

- **Foundation preparation** — excavate to the design level, proof-roll, and
  verify the foundation soil and the levelling pad.
- **Facing erection** — control the **verticality**, **horizontal alignment**, and
  **joint widths** of the facing; these are the tolerances most visible in the
  finished structure and the ones most often missed.
- **Reinforcement placement** — verify the **length**, **orientation**,
  **elevation**, and **spacing** of every layer, that it is not damaged, that it
  is placed on compacted fill (not on loose fill or a slope), and that the
  connection to the facing is made correctly.
- **Backfill placement and compaction** — place fill in lifts and compact it to
  the specified density. Within about **1 m of the face**, use only **lightweight
  compaction equipment** so that the facing and the reinforcement connection are
  not disturbed (Figure 84); larger equipment is permitted farther from the face.
- **Tolerances** — the manual gives out-of-tolerance conditions and their likely
  causes (Table 16), so the inspector can diagnose a problem rather than simply
  record it. Table 15 is the **field inspection checklist**.

## 9.4 Performance monitoring programs

**Purpose of a monitoring program.** Monitoring confirms that the structure
behaves as the design predicted — that the facing deformation, reinforcement
tension, and settlement stay within the assumed values — and gives early warning
if they do not. It applies the **Observational Method**: predictions are made,
measurements are compared with them, and pre-planned actions follow.

**Limited monitoring program.** For routine structures: **visual inspection** of
the facing for cracking, bulging, or joint opening, plus **survey of facing
movement** and settlement of the wall crest. Cheap, and enough for a
well-understood wall on a competent foundation.

**Comprehensive monitoring program.** For tall walls, abutments, walls on soft
foundations, or critical structures, the program adds instrumentation:

- **Inclinometers** — lateral movement of the facing and the ground behind it.
- **Settlement platforms and survey points** — vertical movement of the
  foundation and the reinforced mass.
- **Strain gauges or load cells on the reinforcement** — the tension actually
  mobilized in selected layers, to compare with $T_{max}$.
- **Pressure cells** — contact stress at the base and behind the facing.
- **Piezometers** — pore pressures in the reinforced fill and the foundation,
  which reveal whether drainage is working.

Table 17 lists the possible instruments and what each one measures.

**Program implementation.** Define the **instrument locations**, the **reading
frequencies** (frequent during construction, reducing afterwards), the
**trigger values**, and the **actions** to be taken if a trigger is exceeded —
all before construction begins.

**Data interpretation.** Plot each measurement against time and against the
construction stage, and compare it with the design prediction. A single reading
means little; the **trend** and the **rate** are what matter — the same principle
that governs dam and excavation monitoring elsewhere in this knowledge base.

## 9.5 Key takeaways

- **Inspect materials before they are buried** — reinforcement and drainage
  cannot be inspected later.
- The tolerances that matter most are **facing verticality, alignment, and joint
  width**, and **reinforcement length and elevation**.
- **Compact lightly within 1 m of the face**; heavier equipment farther away.
- Choose the monitoring program by **risk**: limited (visual + survey) for
  routine walls, comprehensive (instrumented) for tall, soft-ground, or critical
  structures.
- Instrument the **reinforcement itself** if you want to know the actual
  mobilized tension — and set **trigger values and actions in advance**.
