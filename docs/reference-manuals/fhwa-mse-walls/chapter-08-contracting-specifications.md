# Chapter 8 — Contracting Methods & Specifications

## 8.1 Policy development

An agency that intends to use MSE walls and reinforced slopes repeatedly needs a
**policy**: which systems are approved, what design criteria apply, who designs
the structure (the agency or the supplier), and how the work is specified and
paid for. Without a policy, every project becomes a negotiation.

## 8.2 System or component approvals

Because many MSE systems are **proprietary**, agencies approve **systems** or
**components** in advance, on the basis of the supplier's design methods, test
data, and performance record. Approval transfers part of the design responsibility
to the supplier and lets the agency specify a class of system rather than a
single product.

## 8.3 Design and performance criteria

The agency states the criteria the structure must meet — geometry, loads,
factors of safety, allowable deformation, design life, and durability — so that
any approved system can be shown to comply.

## 8.4 Agency or supplier design

The manual distinguishes two design responsibilities:

**Agency design** — the agency designs the structure and specifies the
reinforcement and facing explicitly. The design package includes:

- **Plan and elevation sheets** — geometry, limits, and layout.
- **Facing/panel details** — panel or block type, dimensions, and connections.
- **Drainage facilities / special details** — outlets, filters, and terminations.
- **Design computations** — the external, internal, and global stability checks.
- **Geotechnical report** — the soil parameters and the basis of design.
- **Construction specifications** — the material and workmanship requirements.

**Supplier (end-result) design** — the agency specifies the *result* (geometry,
performance, and design criteria) and the supplier designs and warrants the
system.

## 8.5 End-result design approach

In end-result (performance) contracting, the agency specifies:

- **Geometric requirements** — wall lines, heights, and limits.
- **Geotechnical requirements** — the design soil parameters and the fill
  specification.
- **Structural and design requirements** — the loads, factors of safety, and
  design-life/durability rules.
- **Performance requirements** — the allowable deformation, differential
  settlement, and any monitoring obligations.

The supplier then chooses a system and provides the design, with the agency
reviewing and approving it.

## 8.6 Standard designs

**MSEW standard designs.** Pre-worked wall geometries and reinforcement layouts
for common heights and conditions (from [Chapter 4](chapter-04-design-mse-walls.md)).

**RSS standard designs.** Equivalent pre-worked reinforced-slope designs (from
[Chapter 6](chapter-06-rss-project-evaluation.md)).

Standard designs let routine structures be built without a bespoke design, and
give the contractor a known quantity to price.

## 8.7 Review and approvals

The manual describes the review process: submittal requirements, the technical
review of the supplier's design, and the approvals needed before construction.

## 8.8 Construction specifications and special provisions for MSEW and RSS

The manual provides **model specification language** — material requirements,
construction procedures, tolerances, and measurement-and-payment provisions —
that an agency can adapt.

## 8.9–8.12 Guide specifications

The manual's guide specifications cover:

- **MSE walls with segmental precast concrete facings** — panels, connections,
  bearing pads, and joint materials.
- **Concrete modular block (MBW) facing and unit fill** — block units, pins or
  connectors, and the unit fill behind the facing.
- **Geosynthetic reinforcement materials** — polymer type, strength, reduction
  factors, and testing requirements.
- **Reinforced slope systems** — RSS construction (agency design), **erosion
  control mats or blankets**, **geosynthetic drainage composites**, and
  geosynthetic reinforced soil slope systems.

## 8.13 Key takeaways

- Decide **who designs** — agency or supplier — before writing the contract; it
  determines the whole submittal and review process.
- **Approve systems or components in advance** so the contract can specify a
  class of system, not a single proprietary product.
- **Standard designs** convert routine walls and slopes into known quantities.
- The **guide specifications** are the manual's most directly reusable output —
  adapt them, do not rewrite them.
