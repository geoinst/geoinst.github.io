# MSE Walls & Reinforced Soil Slopes — FHWA Design & Construction Guidelines

!!! note "About this reference"
    This is an **original, open technical reference** on mechanically stabilized
    earth (MSE) walls and reinforced soil slopes (RSS), written in plain
    engineering language. It is adapted from the subject scope and structure of
    **FHWA-NHI-00-043, *Mechanically Stabilized Earth Walls and Reinforced Soil
    Slopes — Design & Construction Guidelines*** (March 2001), prepared by
    **Victor Elias, P.E.; Barry R. Christopher, Ph.D., P.E.; and Ryan R. Berg,
    P.E.** for the Federal Highway Administration's **National Highway Institute**
    (NHI Course No. 132042, Office of Bridge Technology). The source is a
    **U.S. Government work in the public domain** (*Distribution Statement: No
    restrictions*); this page is nevertheless an independent adaptation, **not** a
    verbatim reproduction. It is provided for education and field use, and every
    project is unique: the guidance here builds engineering judgment, it does not
    set minimum standards. The full manual is indexed in the
    [GTI Doctor](../../gti-doctor.md) AI assistant for deeper queries.

The **FHWA *Mechanically Stabilized Earth Walls and Reinforced Soil Slopes*
manual** is the design and construction reference that carries reinforced-soil
technology from first principles to a buildable highway structure. Where the
[FHWA instrumentation manual](../fhwa/index.md) tells you how to *watch* an earth
structure, this manual tells you how to *design and build* one: how frictional
soil and tensile reinforcement are combined into a composite that behaves as a
gravity mass, and how that composite is proportioned, detailed, specified, and
inspected on a transportation project.

The manual is the reference text for the NHI course of the same name. It was
written to let an engineer identify and evaluate MSE wall and RSS applications as
an alternative to conventional construction, and its scope is deliberately broad
enough to serve specification writers, construction inspectors, and contracting
personnel as well as designers.

This reference follows the manual's arc: **project evaluation** (when and why to
use reinforced soil); **soil reinforcement principles** (how the composite
carries load); **design** of MSE walls, complex-geometry walls, and reinforced
slopes; **contracting methods and specifications**; and **field inspection and
performance monitoring** — closing with the manual's five appendices on pullout
factors, creep extrapolation, costs, reinforcement dimensions, and a worked RSS
analysis.

## How to use this reference

- **Start with Chapter 2 if you are choosing a system**, and Chapter 3 if you need
  the mechanics. Chapters 4, 5, and 7 are the design procedures.
- **Then read the chapter for your structure.** MSE walls (Chapters 4–5) and
  reinforced soil slopes (Chapters 6–7) are different structures with different
  failure modes, even though they share the same reinforcement principles.
- **Use it with the rest of the knowledge base.** The design chapters cross-reference
  the [Foundation Engineering](../foundation-engineering/index.md) treatment of
  earth pressure and retaining walls, and the [FHWA instrumentation manual](../fhwa/index.md)
  and [Dunnicliff](../dunnicliff/index.md) for the monitoring that Chapter 9 calls
  for.
- **Ask the [GTI Doctor](../../gti-doctor.md).** The AI assistant can answer
  structure-specific questions from the full FHWA corpus.

## The central idea

Reinforced soil is not a wall with a facing; it is a **composite material**. A
frictional fill, which can carry almost no tension, is combined with
reinforcement elements that can carry almost no compression. Together they form a
mass that behaves as if it had an *apparent cohesion* — strong enough to stand at
a steep angle, or vertically as a wall, without the mass concrete or heavy
structural elements of a conventional retaining wall.

> **Friction carries the load.** The reinforcement is not anchored to a rigid
> structure; it is *gripped by the soil*. Every design equation in this manual —
> pullout capacity, connection strength, internal stability — is ultimately a
> statement about mobilizing friction between soil and reinforcement.
