# Chapter 11 — Rockbolts, Cablebolts & Shotcrete Support Systems

## 11.1 Fundamental Support Mechanisms

Ground support systems stabilize excavations through three distinct mechanical mechanisms:

1. **Suspension**: Suspending dead-weight rock blocks or loose stratified layers across unstable spans into competent overlying rock strata.
2. **Beam Building (Lamination Clamping)**: Bolting thin, stratified sedimentary roof beds together creates a composite structural beam. Clamping normal stresses generate inter-bed shear friction, suppressing slip and reducing roof deflection by a factor of $n^2$ (where $n$ is the number of bolted laminations).
3. **Reinforced Rock Arch (Keying & Confinement)**: In blocky or heavily fractured rock, pattern bolting with surface shotcrete applies radial confining stress ($\sigma_3 > 0$). This mobilizes the Hoek-Brown strength envelope, transforming broken rock into a self-supporting structural compression ring.


| Bolt Type | Anchorage Mechanism | Load-Deformation Characteristic | Primary Application |
|-----------|---------------------|---------------------------------|---------------------|
| **Mechanically Anchored Expansion Shell** | Point anchor wedged into rock by serrated shell | Stiff initial elastic response; susceptible to blast vibration loosening | Temporary mining support in hard, competent rock |
| **Fully Grouted Resin Rebar** | Continuous bond along corrugated steel rebar using polyester resin capsules | High stiffness ($> 100\text{ kN/mm}$); rapid cure ($30\text{ s}$); excellent corrosion protection | Civil tunnels and permanent underground mining roadways |
| **Friction Stabilizer (Split Set)** | C-shaped slotted steel tube driven into undersized hole ($38\text{ mm}$ into $35\text{ mm}$ hole) | Frictional yield along length ($10 - 15\text{ kN/m}$ embedment); large displacement capacity | High-deformation mining stopes; temporary sidewall pinning |
| **Inflatable Tube (Swellex)** | Folded steel tube expanded hydraulically with high-pressure water ($30\text{ MPa}$) | Immediate full-column mechanical interlock; high capacity ($100 - 200\text{ kN}$) with moderate ductility | Rapid installation in tunneling; water-bearing rock masses |
| **Yielding Rockbolt (D-Bolt, Garford)** | Smooth rebar with engineered anchor wedges or upset points | Deforms up to $150 - 200\text{ mm}$ under constant high load ($180 - 220\text{ kN}$) | Rockburst-prone and severe squeezing ground environments |

## 11.3 Steel Fiber Reinforced Shotcrete (SFRS)

Shotcrete (sprayed concrete) acts as a flexible, fast-acting structural skin.

### Advantages of Steel Fiber Reinforced Shotcrete:
1. **Eliminates Wire Mesh Hazard**: Spraying fibers directly eliminates the time-consuming and dangerous manual installation of welded wire mesh against unsupported rock faces.
2. **Post-Crack Toughness**: While unreinforced shotcrete fails brittlely immediately upon cracking, ductile steel fibers ($30 - 50\text{ kg/m}^3$ mix) bridge crack mouths, providing substantial energy absorption capacity ($Q$-system energy absorption classes: $500\text{ J}, 700\text{ J}, 1000\text{ J}$).
3. **Early Strength Gain**: Accelerator admixtures achieve compressive strengths of $1\text{ MPa}$ within 1 hour and $10\text{ MPa}$ within 24 hours, providing immediate support behind advancing drill-and-blast or TBM headings.

## 11.4 Support Interaction & System Compatibility

The most common failure mode of ground support systems is **strain incompatibility**:

- Pairing a stiff, brittle support (unreinforced thick shotcrete) with a flexible rockbolt (Split Set) causes the shotcrete to crack and spall at $2\text{ mm}$ deformation, long before the bolts mobilize their tensile capacity.
- **The Golden Rule of Support Design**: Ground support components (surface skin, tendon, anchor) must possess matching displacement capacities and deform harmoniously under the anticipated rock mass dilation.
