# Practical Rock Engineering (Dr. Evert Hoek)

!!! info "Open Access & Educational Reference"
    **Practical Rock Engineering** by Dr. Evert Hoek is a world-renowned, open educational compendium created for practicing civil and mining geotechnical engineers, geologists, and students. Originally prepared as lecture notes and updated continuously via [Rocscience Hoek's Corner](https://www.rocscience.com/learning/hoeks-corner), this reference bridges theoretical geomechanics with real-world design, tunneling, excavation support, and open pit mine slopes.
    
    *Source: Dr. Evert Hoek, Rocscience Open Educational Resource.*

## Overview & Significance

Dr. Evert Hoek is one of the foundational figures of modern rock engineering. Co-originator of the **Hoek-Brown failure criterion** and the **Geological Strength Index (GSI)**, his work revolutionized how engineers analyze heavily jointed, fractured rock masses that do not behave like isotropic laboratory rock cylinders.

In mining and civil engineering, *Practical Rock Engineering* provides the definitive design methods for:
1. **Intact Rock Strength & Failure Criteria**: Deriving non-linear empirical shear strength envelopes from triaxial and uniaxial laboratory tests.
2. **Rock Mass Classification & GSI**: Quantifying rock mass structure (blockiness) and joint surface conditions to estimate equivalent continuum properties.
3. **Underground Excavations & Caverns**: Analyzing induced stresses, squeezing ground, structurally controlled block fallouts, and designing rockbolt, cablebolt, and shotcrete support systems.
4. **Rock Slopes in Civil & Open Pit Mining**: Analyzing planar, wedge, circular, and toppling failures, calculating factor of safety ($FS$) and probability of failure ($P_f$), and designing depressurization drainage.
5. **Blasting & Controlled Excavation**: Minimizing blast damage to highwalls and tunnel perimeters.

## Integration with ADAQS & Geotechnical Monitoring

*Practical Rock Engineering* emphasizes that analytical and numerical predictions must always be verified in the field:
- **Displacement verification**: Extensometers (MPBX) and convergence tape stations measure whether rock deformation follows the Ground Reaction Curve (GRC).
- **Slope radar & InSAR**: Detects tertiary creep acceleration on open pit benches where $FS$ approaches unity.
- **Microseismic arrays**: Listens to acoustic emissions and micro-fracturing around deep mining stopes to forecast rockburst events.
- **Pore pressure control**: Vibrating wire piezometers verify that sub-horizontal drains successfully draw down joint water pressures.
