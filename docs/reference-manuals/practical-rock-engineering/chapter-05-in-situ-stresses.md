# Chapter 5 — In-Situ & Induced Stresses Around Underground Openings

## 5.1 The Virgin In-Situ Stress Field

Before an excavation is created, the subterranean rock mass is subjected to a state of natural equilibrium termed the **virgin (or in-situ) stress field**.

### Vertical Stress ($\sigma_v$)
Vertical stress is generated by the gravity weight of the overlying rock column (overburden depth $z$, unit weight $\gamma \approx 0.027\text{ MN/m}^3$):

$$\sigma_v = \int_0^z \gamma(z)\, dz \approx \gamma z \approx 0.027 z \quad (\text{MPa for } z \text{ in meters})$$

### Horizontal Stress Ratio ($k_0$)
Tectonic plate motions, thermal contraction, and erosion history cause horizontal stresses ($\sigma_h, \sigma_H$) to deviate sharply from simple hydrostatic assumptions:

$$k_0 = \frac{\sigma_h}{\sigma_v}$$

Based on worldwide measurements compiled by Hoek and Brown, at shallow depths ($< 500\text{ m}$), $k_0$ frequently exceeds $1.5$ to $3.5$ due to locked-in tectonic remnant stresses:

$$\frac{100}{z} + 0.3 \le k_0 \le \frac{1500}{z} + 0.5$$

## 5.2 The Kirsch Analytical Solution for Circular Tunnels

Excavating an underground cavity eliminates internal tractions, forcing stresses to flow around the void. For a circular tunnel of radius $a$ in an isotropic elastic medium subjected to vertical stress $p$ and horizontal stress $k p$:

### Tangential (Hoop) Stress ($\sigma_\theta$)

$$\sigma_\theta = \frac{p}{2} \left[ (1 + k)\left(1 + \frac{a^2}{r^2}\right) - (1 - k)\left(1 + 3\frac{a^4}{r^4}\right)\cos 2\theta \right]$$

### Radial Stress ($\sigma_r$)

$$\sigma_r = \frac{p}{2} \left[ (1 + k)\left(1 - \frac{a^2}{r^2}\right) + (1 - k)\left(1 - 4\frac{a^2}{r^2} + 3\frac{a^4}{r^4}\right)\cos 2\theta \right]$$

### At the Tunnel Boundary ($r = a$):
- Radial stress is zero: $\sigma_r = 0$.
- Crown / Invert ($\theta = \pi/2$): $\sigma_{\theta,\text{crown}} = p (3k - 1)$.
- Sidewalls ($\theta = 0$): $\sigma_{\theta,\text{sidewall}} = p (3 - k)$.

```
Case A: Hydrostatic Stress (k = 1.0)
Boundary Tangential Stress: sigma_theta = 2p everywhere (2x concentration).

Case B: High Horizontal Tectonic Stress (k = 2.0)
Crown Stress: sigma_theta = p(3(2) - 1) = 5p  (Severe compressive crushing / spalling).
Sidewall Stress: sigma_theta = p(3 - 2) = 1p.

Case C: Low Horizontal Stress (k = 0.25)
Crown Stress: sigma_theta = p(3(0.25) - 1) = -0.25p (Tensile opening / block dropouts).
Sidewall Stress: sigma_theta = p(3 - 0.25) = 2.75p.
```

## 5.3 Stress Concentration & Failure Modes Around Caverns

When induced tangential stress $\sigma_\theta$ exceeds the rock mass compressive strength ($\sigma_{cm}$):
1. **Brittle Spalling / Slabbing**: Stable thin flakes peel off parallel to the excavation boundary in massive hard rock ($UCS > 100\text{ MPa}$).
2. **Plastic Yielding Zone**: A circular plastic annulus of radius $r_p > a$ develops around the opening, redistributing peak stresses away from the perimeter into the solid interior.
3. **Tensile De-Stressing**: In tensile roof zones, joint friction drops to zero ($\sigma_n' \le 0$), causing unbonded rock blocks to drop under gravity unless secured with pattern rockbolts.

## 5.4 In-Situ Stress Measurement Methods

| Technique | Operating Principle | Depth Range |
|-----------|---------------------|-------------|
| **Overcoring (CSIRO HI Cell)** | Measures stress relief strains when a pilot hole is overcored by a larger diamond barrel | Up to $30 - 50\text{ m}$ from underground faces |
| **Hydraulic Fracturing (Hydrofrac)** | Isolates a borehole interval with straddle packers, pressurizes with water until rock fractures perpendicular to $\sigma_3$ | Surface boreholes $> 1,000\text{ m}$ |
| **Borehole Breakout Analysis** | Caliper and acoustic televiewer logs detect compressive spalling diametrally aligned with minimum horizontal stress direction | Deep exploration drillholes |
