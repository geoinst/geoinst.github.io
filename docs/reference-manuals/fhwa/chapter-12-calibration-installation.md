# Chapter 12 — Calibration, Maintenance & Installation

## 12.1 Introduction

The best-planned program fails if the instruments are not installed and maintained
correctly. This chapter covers the field tasks that keep instruments trustworthy:
**acceptance testing, calibration, maintenance, and installation**, and the records
that document them.

## 12.2 Acceptance testing

- **Pre-installation acceptance tests** (Table 12-1) — performed on instruments
  before installation to confirm they are within specification (for example, zero
  stability, calibration check, and electrical continuity). An instrument that
  fails here must not be installed.
- **Post-installation acceptance tests** — performed after installation to confirm
  the instrument survived installation and reads sensibly against a known
  condition.

## 12.3 Calibration

Calibration establishes the relationship between the instrument's raw signal and
the engineering quantity it measures. Key points:

- Calibrate **before installation** and **periodically** thereafter, as part of the
  planning process ([Chapter 6](chapter-06-planning.md)).
- Record the **calibration factor, zero, and span** and carry them through every
  reading.
- Watch for **drift** — a slow change in zero or span over time.

## 12.4 Maintenance

Instruments degrade. Planned maintenance includes:

- protecting **tubes and cables** where they emerge from a borehole;
- checking **desiccant** in enclosures and **lightning protection**;
- verifying **power and communications** for automated systems; and
- replacing consumables before they fail.

## 12.5 Installation

Installation determines whether the instrument measures what it is supposed to:

- **Drilling** must not smear or disturb the ground around a piezometer or
  inclinometer casing.
- **Grouting and sealing** must isolate the measurement zone.
- **Tubes and cables** must be protected against construction traffic and
  settlement (Figure 12-1 shows typical protection arrangements).
- **Installation record sheets** (Table 12-2) document as-built position, depth,
  seal, and the calibration values in force — the permanent record that makes the
  data interpretable years later.

## 12.6 Key takeaways

- **Acceptance-test every instrument** before and after installation.
- Calibration defines the **zero and span** — record and carry them through.
- **Maintain** instruments on a planned schedule; do not wait for failure.
- **Installation records** are as important as the readings — they make the data
  interpretable later.
- Protect **tubes and cables**: the most common cause of automated-system failure.
