# Chapter 10 — Instrumentation for Ground Improvement & Temperature

## 10.1 Introduction

**Ground improvement** changes the properties of the ground in place — by grouting,
densification, or drainage — to make it suitable for construction. Instrumentation
confirms that the improvement has achieved its purpose and that the surrounding
ground is not adversely affected. This chapter also covers **temperature
measurement**, which is both a ground-improvement monitoring parameter (for example,
ground freezing) and a correction input for other instruments.

## 10.2 Monitoring grouting

**General role.** Grouting fills voids and fractures and densifies or strengthens
the ground. Instrumentation monitors the spread of grout, the resulting ground
movement, and the effect on adjacent structures.

**Principal geotechnical questions:**

- Where has the grout gone, and how far has it spread?
- Has the grouting caused heave or movement of adjacent structures?
- Have the intended improvements in permeability or stiffness been achieved?

**Suitable instruments** (Table 10-1) typically include: **piezometers** (pore
pressure response), **inclinometers** and **settlement/heave points** (ground
movement), **pressure** instruments (injection pressure), and **permeability
tests**.

## 10.3 Temperature measurement

Temperature is measured to:

- monitor **ground freezing** and other thermal ground-improvement techniques;
- confirm the setting/hydration of grout or concrete; and
- provide **thermal corrections** for vibrating-wire and strain instruments.

**Instruments** (Table 10-2) include **thermistors**, **RTDs**, **thermocouples**,
and **vibrating-wire temperature sensors**, compared on range, accuracy, and ease of
logging. Choose based on the required range and the ability to share the datalogger
with the primary instruments.

## 10.4 Key takeaways

- Instrumentation confirms that ground improvement **achieved its purpose** and did
  no harm to adjacent ground.
- **Grouting** is monitored with piezometers, movement instruments, and injection
  pressures.
- **Temperature** is both a monitoring parameter (ground freezing) and a
  **correction input** for other instruments.
- Record temperature wherever thermal effects could bias strain or deformation.
