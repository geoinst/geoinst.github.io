# Chapter 3 — Overview of Hardware for Deformation

## 3.1 Introduction

Deformation is the parameter that most directly reveals whether the ground or a
structure is behaving as predicted. Instruments for measuring deformation span a
wide range, from simple surface crack gages to deep borehole inclinometers. They
are grouped into categories (surface vs. subsurface; discrete vs. profile) so that
the right instrument can be matched to the geometry of the expected movement.

## 3.2 Surveying methods

**Optical and GPS surveying** measure the movement of surface points — settlement,
heave, and horizontal displacement — relative to stable reference monuments. They
give a broad, low-cost overview and are often the first line of deformation
monitoring, but they cannot see movement at depth.

## 3.3 Crack gages

Crack gages monitor the opening or closing of a single crack or joint:

- **Mechanical types:** pins-and-tape, pins with a steel rule or calipers,
  pins with tensioned wire and weight, the **grid crack monitor**, mechanical
  strain gage, and dial indicator.
- **Electrical types:** transducers that convert crack movement to an electrical
  signal for logging.

## 3.4 Convergence gages

**Convergence gages** measure the change in distance between two points on a
structure — for example, the narrowing of a tunnel or the closure of an excavation.

## 3.5 Tiltmeters

A **tiltmeter** measures rotation of a surface or structure from the change in
orientation of a sensor. Two common transducer types are:

- **Accelerometer-based tiltmeters** — robust, used for structural and ground
  tilt.
- **Electrolytic-level tiltmeters** — very high resolution for small rotations.

## 3.6 Probe extensometers

A **probe extensometer** measures displacement at discrete depths along a borehole
by lowering a probe to magnetic or inductive targets:

- **Induction-coil gages** — a probe detects the position of metal targets.
- **Magnet / reed-switch gages** — a probe detects ring magnets grouted at chosen
  depths.

## 3.7 Settlement platforms and subsurface settlement points

- **Settlement platforms** — surface plates (often with risers) that record fill
  settlement as embankments are built.
- **Subsurface settlement points** — anchors placed at depth, either a **driven or
  grouted anchor** or a **Borros anchor**, to record settlement of a buried layer.

## 3.8 Fixed borehole extensometers and series extensometers

- **Fixed borehole extensometers** — permanently installed anchors with rods or
  wires to a reference head, giving continuous measurement of the distance between
  fixed depths.
- **Series extensometers** — a stack of anchors in one borehole that resolves
  deformation into **increments between adjacent anchors**, useful for locating the
  zone of movement.

## 3.9 Inclinometers

The **inclinometer** is the workhorse for measuring horizontal deformation with
depth — in slopes, embankments, excavations, and around foundations. A probe with
two orthogonal servo-accelerometers travels along a grooved casing, and the
accumulated tilt gives the deflection profile.

Variants include:

- **Horizontal inclinometers** — measure settlement profiles along a horizontal
  casing.
- **In-place (fixed) inclinometers** — a string of sensors left in the casing for
  continuous, automated monitoring.
- **Shear-plane indicators** — detect the depth at which a casing shears.

## 3.10 Liquid-level gages

**Liquid-level (settlement) gages** use a liquid-filled tube connected to a cell to
measure settlement or heave by the change in pressure or liquid level:

- **Single-point gages** — with both ends at the same elevation, with the readout
  unit higher than the cell, or with the cell and tube installed within a borehole.
- **Full-profile gages** — a series of cells giving a complete settlement profile.

## 3.11 Telltales and slurry-trench convergence gages

- **Telltales** — simple rods or wires anchored at depth that measure movement of a
  specific point relative to the surface; **multiple telltales** resolve movement
  between several points.
- **Convergence gages for slurry trenches** — hydraulic gages that measure the
  convergence of a trench during construction.

## 3.12 Metallic time-domain reflectometry (TDR)

**Metallic TDR** sends a pulse down a coaxial cable and detects reflections from
kinks or breaks caused by ground movement. It is a low-cost way to detect and
locate **shear deformation** in soil and rock.

## 3.13 Acoustic emission monitoring

**Acoustic emission (AE)** monitoring detects the high-frequency sound generated
when soil or rock grains slip and break. Counting emission events or the count
rate gives early warning of developing instability.

## 3.14 Key takeaways

- Deformation instruments range from **surface survey** to **deep borehole**
  profiling.
- **Inclinometers** are the standard for horizontal movement with depth;
  **extensometers** for vertical or axial movement.
- **Series extensometers** and **multiple telltales** resolve movement *between*
  anchors to locate the active zone.
- **TDR** and **acoustic emission** are low-cost early-warning tools.
- Match the instrument's **range and resolution** to the magnitude of movement you
  expect.
