# Chapter 4 — Overview of Hardware for Total Stress in Soil

## 4.1 Introduction

Total stress is the sum of effective stress and pore pressure ($\sigma = \sigma' +
u$). Because pore pressure is measured separately with piezometers
([Chapter 2](chapter-02-groundwater-pressure.md)), measuring **total stress** lets
the engineer separate the two and obtain effective stress — the quantity that
actually governs strength and deformation. Total stress in soil is measured with
**earth pressure cells**.

## 4.2 Embedment earth pressure cells

An **embedment earth pressure cell** is a flat, fluid- or diaphragm-filled pad
embedded in the soil that measures the total stress acting normal to its face.

### 4.2.1 Typical cells

The cell consists of two plates welded at the edges with a thin layer of fluid
between them; total stress deflects the plates and is read by a pressure
transducer (frequently **vibrating-wire**). The cell must be **embedded** so that
the soil acts on it as it would on the surrounding ground.

### 4.2.2 Limitations imposed by the soil environment

Because a cell is stiffer or softer than the soil around it, it **redistributes**
stress locally, so the cell measures a stress that may differ from the true free-field
value. This is the central problem of earth pressure measurement.

### 4.2.3 Factors affecting measurements

The main factors are:

- the **aspect ratio** (diameter-to-thickness ratio) of the cell,
- the **ratio of soil stiffness to cell stiffness**,
- the quality of **placement and backfill** around the cell, and
- temperature and calibration effects.

A high aspect ratio and a cell stiffness matched to the soil both reduce the error.

## 4.3 Contact earth pressure cells

A **contact earth pressure cell** measures stress (or load) at an interface — the
contact between soil and a structure, or at the base of a foundation element.
Variants include:

- **Standard contact cells** — soil/structure interface stress.
- **Cells for load at the toe of driven piles** — placed at the pile tip.
- **Cells for load at the base of drilled shafts** — mounted between thick circular
  plates.
- **Cells for stress on the sides of driven piles** — to separate shaft friction
  from end bearing.
- **Cells for measuring stress on the sides of drilled shafts and other contact
  surfaces.**

Because the structure's stiffness differs from the soil's, contact cells also
require careful placement and interpretation.

## 4.4 Key takeaways

- **Total stress + pore pressure = effective stress.** Measuring both separates the
  strength-controlling quantity.
- Earth pressure cells **redistribute** local stress; a mismatch in stiffness causes
  error.
- **Aspect ratio** and **soil/cell stiffness ratio** are the two key design factors
  for embedment cells.
- **Contact cells** resolve stress at interfaces and at pile toes and shaft bases.
- Placement quality is as important as the instrument itself.
