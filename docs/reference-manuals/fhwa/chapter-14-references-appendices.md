# Chapter 14 — References & Appendices

## 14.1 References

The FHWA manual's reference chapter points the reader to the wider literature on
geotechnical instrumentation. The **primary source** of text and figures for the
manual is:

> Dunnicliff, J. (1988, 1993). *Geotechnical Instrumentation for Monitoring Field
> Performance.* John Wiley & Sons, Inc., New York, 577 p.

That work — and the complementary NCHRP Synthesis 89 — are the deeper references
behind this module, and are indexed in the [GTI Doctor](../../gti-doctor.md) AI
assistant. The instrument-by-instrument coverage in this site's
[Dunnicliff reference](../dunnicliff/index.md) complements this manual.

## 14.2 Appendices — the planning toolkit

The manual's appendices are its most practical feature: they turn the planning
process into worked examples and checklists.

### Appendix A — Planning checklist

A practical checklist for planning a geotechnical instrumentation program: the
steps, questions, and deliverables that make up the planning process of
[Chapter 6](chapter-06-planning.md).

### Appendix B — Planning steps, illustrated

Step-by-step illustrations of the planning process:

- **B-1 to B-7** — define project conditions; predict mechanisms; define questions;
  define the purpose; select parameters; predict magnitudes; the task-assignment
  chart.
- **B-8 to B-10** — assign tasks; select instruments.
- **B-11 to B-16** — instrument illustrations: open standpipe piezometer (push-in),
  vibrating-wire piezometer (push-in), magnet/reed-switch probe extensometer,
  settlement platform, liquid-level gage, and inclinometer.
- **B-17 to B-18** — select instrument locations; possible instrument locations.
- **B-19 to B-20** — plan recording of factors that may influence measured data.
- **B-21 to B-22** — establish procedures for ensuring reading correctness.
- **B-23 to B-28** — prepare the system design report; write procurement
  specifications; plan installation; plan calibration and maintenance; write
  specifications for field services.

### Appendix C — Soil-slope planning examples

Worked examples for monitoring a **cut slope in soil** and a **cut slope in rock**
— the results of the student exercise in [Chapter 7](chapter-07-soil-slopes-embankments.md).

### Appendix D — Deep-foundation examples

Worked examples for load-transfer instrumentation:

- **D-1** — possible behavior mechanisms of a driven pile.
- **D-2, D-3** — series extensometer and multiple telltales.
- **D-4** — strain/stress conversion.
- **D-5, D-6** — static axial load tests of a steel pile and a concrete pile **with**
  comprehensive load-transfer data.
- **D-7** — static axial test **without** comprehensive load-transfer data.
- **D-8** — axial load test of a drilled shaft.

### Appendix E — MSE wall examples

Worked examples for a mechanically stabilized earth wall: possible instrumentation
for **comprehensive stability monitoring** and for **determining the magnitude and
distribution of stress** — the results of the exercise in
[Chapter 9](chapter-09-earth-retaining.md).

### Appendix F — Data-evaluation examples

Suggested evaluations for the student exercise in
[Chapter 13](chapter-13-data-interpretation.md):

- **F-1** — optical survey data.
- **F-2** — correct inclinometer data.

## 14.3 Key takeaways

- The **primary reference** behind this module is Dunnicliff's textbook — go there
  for depth.
- The appendices are the **practical heart** of the manual: checklists, planning
  steps, and worked examples.
- Use **Appendix A** as your planning checklist and **Appendices C–F** as templates
  for your own projects.
- The [GTI Doctor](../../gti-doctor.md) can retrieve the underlying corpus on
  demand.
