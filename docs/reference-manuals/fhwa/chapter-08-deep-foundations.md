# Chapter 8 — Instrumentation for Deep Foundations

## 8.1 Introduction

Deep foundations — **driven piles** and **drilled shafts** — carry load from a
structure to the ground at depth. Instrumentation during **load tests** and during
construction answers the central question: *how is the load distributed between
shaft friction and end bearing, and how does the foundation behave under load?*

## 8.2 Driven piles

**General role.** Instrumentation during a static axial load test measures the
**load-transfer** behavior of the pile: how load passes from the pile into the soil
along its length and at its toe.

**Principal geotechnical questions:**

- How is axial load distributed between shaft and toe?
- What is the mobilized shaft friction and end bearing at working load?
- Is the pile behaving elastically, and is the measured load consistent with the
  applied load?

**Suitable instruments** (Table 8-1) typically include: **strain gages** or
**sister bars** (axial strain → load), **tell tales / multiple telltales**
(displacement), **load cells** at the pile head and toe, and **extensometers**
(internal settlement).

## 8.3 Drilled shafts

**General role.** As with driven piles, instrumentation resolves load transfer and
defines the load–settlement response. Drilled shafts permit instrumentation to be
installed in the wet concrete or the reinforcing cage.

**Principal geotechnical questions:**

- What is the load–settlement response of the shaft?
- How is load shared between side resistance and base resistance?
- Is the base clean and behaving as assumed?

**Suitable instruments** (Table 8-2) typically include: **sister bars** (strain →
load in the cage), **contact earth pressure cells** at the base, **multiple
telltales** (interval movement), and **load cells**.

## 8.4 Interpreting load-transfer data

Converting measured strain to load uses the member's **elastic modulus**
($P = E A \varepsilon$). A useful check is a **cause-and-effect** plot: the measured
load at a level should be consistent with the applied load minus the shaft load
above it. When "comprehensive load transfer data" are available, the distribution
of shaft friction and end bearing can be separated directly; when they are not, only
the total response can be inferred. Worked examples appear in **Appendix D** (see
[Chapter 14](chapter-14-references-appendices.md)).

## 8.5 Key takeaways

- Instrument deep-foundation load tests to resolve **shaft vs. toe** load.
- **Sister bars** and **strain gages** convert strain to load via the **elastic
  modulus**.
- **Telltales** and **extensometers** give the displacement profile.
- Use a **cause-and-effect check** to confirm the data are physically consistent.
- Worked load-transfer examples are in **Appendix D**.
