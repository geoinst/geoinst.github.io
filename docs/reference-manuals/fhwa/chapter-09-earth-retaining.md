# Chapter 9 — Instrumentation for Earth Retaining Structures

## 9.1 Introduction

Earth retaining structures hold back soil or rock and transfer the resulting loads
to the ground or to internal reinforcement. Instrumentation confirms that the
structure is behaving as designed — that movements are small, loads are as
predicted, and the ground behind the wall is stable. This chapter covers braced
excavations, slurry walls, mechanically stabilized earth (MSE) walls, and soil
nailing walls.

## 9.2 Internally braced excavations

**General role.** Instrumentation monitors wall movement, ground movement, strut
loads, and pore pressures around an excavation, so that bracing can be adjusted and
adjacent structures protected.

**Principal geotechnical questions:**

- How much is the wall and the surrounding ground moving?
- What loads are the struts carrying?
- Is the base heaving or is pore pressure building?

**Suitable instruments** (Table 9-1) typically include: **inclinometers** (wall and
ground movement), **load cells** (strut loads), **piezometers** (pore pressure),
**settlement points**, and **survey**.

## 9.3 Externally braced excavations

**General role.** For anchored or tie-back systems, instrumentation measures anchor
loads and ground movement.

**Suitable instruments** (Table 9-2) typically include: **load cells** (anchor
loads), **inclinometers**, **piezometers**, and **survey points**.

## 9.4 Slurry walls

**General role.** Instrumentation confirms the stability of the trench during
construction and the performance of the completed wall.

**Suitable instruments** (Table 9-3) typically include: **convergence gages**
(trench stability), **inclinometers** (wall movement), and **stress/strain** and
**pressure** instruments.

## 9.5 Mechanically stabilized earth (MSE) walls

**General role.** Instrumentation measures the stresses and strains in the
reinforcement and the movement of the facing and the retained soil.

**Principal geotechnical questions:**

- What tension is mobilized in the reinforcement?
- How much does the wall face and the retained soil move?
- What stresses act at the base?

**Suitable instruments** (Table 9-4) typically include: **strain gages / load
cells** on the reinforcement, **inclinometers** and **survey** for movement, and
**pressure cells** at the base.

## 9.6 Soil nailing walls

**General role.** Instrumentation measures nail loads, facing movement, and ground
deformation.

**Suitable instruments** (Table 9-5) typically include: **load cells** on nails,
**inclinometers**, and **survey points**.

## 9.8 Key takeaways

- Match the instrument to the load path: **struts/anchors → load cells**;
  **wall/ground → inclinometers and survey**; **ground → piezometers**.
- For **MSE walls**, instrument the **reinforcement** to see the mobilized tension.
- Protect adjacent structures by monitoring **ground movement outside the
  excavation**.
- Worked MSE-wall examples are in **Appendix E**.
