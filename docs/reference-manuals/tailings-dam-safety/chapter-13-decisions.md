# Chapter 13 — Performance Evaluation and Decision Making

## 13.1 From anomaly to action

Monitoring exists to support decisions. A well-run program defines, *in advance*,
what each level of each parameter means and what must happen next. This removes
hesitation during an event.

## 13.2 Trigger, action, and alarm levels

Most plans define tiered levels:

- **Normal / green** — within expected range; routine monitoring.
- **Alert / yellow** — deviation warrants increased frequency and review.
- **Action / orange** — predefined response: notify engineer, restrict access,
  increase surveillance, prepare response.
- **Alarm / red** — predefined emergency response: evacuate downstream,
  notify regulator, activate emergency plan.

Levels are set per parameter and per location, informed by design and
failure-mode analysis ([Chapter 2](chapter-02-facilities-and-failures.md)).

## 13.3 Decision support, not replacement

Instruments and dashboards **support** the responsible engineer's judgment; they
do not replace it. A reading in the green does not excuse a visible crack; a
single yellow does not mandate evacuation. The plan documents who decides and how.

## 13.4 Emergency response

When red levels or visible distress occur, the response is operational, not just
technical:

- **Evacuation** of downstream populations and on-site personnel per the
  emergency plan.
- **Notification** of regulator and stakeholders.
- **Containment** where safely possible (lower pond, divert flow).
- **Documentation** of actions and observations for later review.

## 13.5 Independent review

Higher-consequence facilities benefit from **independent periodic review** of the
monitoring data and the program itself — a fresh set of eyes on trends the
operating team may have normalized.

!!! danger "Act on acceleration"
    History shows catastrophic tailings failures are often preceded by
    accelerating movement or pore-pressure rise that was observed but not acted
    upon. Predefined action levels exist precisely to force a response before a
    threshold is crossed.

## 13.6 Key takeaways

- Define trigger/action/alarm levels in advance, per parameter and location.
- Monitoring supports — never replaces — responsible engineering judgment.
- Predefine emergency response: evacuate, notify, contain, document.
- Independent review catches normalized trends.
