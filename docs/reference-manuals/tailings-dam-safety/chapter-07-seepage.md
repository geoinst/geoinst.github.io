# Chapter 7 — Seepage and Phreatic-Surface Monitoring

## 7.1 Seepage: friend and foe

Some seepage is normal at a tailings facility; **uncontrolled or changing**
seepage is a leading indicator of internal erosion (piping) and over-saturation.
The surveillance task is to quantify seepage and detect change.

## 7.2 What to measure

- **Location and clarity** — where seepage emerges and whether it is clear or
  turbid (turbidity can signal internal erosion).
- **Discharge** — volume per unit time, via V-notch weirs, flumes, or flow meters.
- **Temperature and chemistry** — can distinguish sources and detect leaks from
  decants or pipelines.
- **Phreatic surface** — the level of the water table within the deposit and
  embankment, tracked by piezometers ([Chapter 6](chapter-06-pore-pressure.md)).

## 7.3 Beach geometry and the phreatic surface

The **beach** is the gently sloping surface where slurry is deposited. Its
geometry controls where the phreatic surface sits. A well-run facility keeps the
phreatic surface **low and within the beach**, far from the crest, by managing
deposition and decant. Monitoring includes:

- **Beach slope and length** (survey, drone photogrammetry).
- **Phreatic-surface elevation** from piezometers and observation wells.
- **Freeboard** — the vertical distance from the pond to the crest.

!!! danger "Beach management is surveillance"
    A short or flat beach pushes the phreatic surface toward the embankment and
    reduces freeboard — a direct path to liquefaction and overtopping. Beach
    geometry is not just operations; it is a safety parameter.

## 7.4 Detecting internal erosion (piping)

Piping is insidious because it can develop inside the embankment. Warning signs:

- New or increasing seepage, especially **turbid** or **sandy** discharge.
- Seepage emerging at unexpected locations (toe, downstream slope).
- Settlement or small depressions over a seepage path.
- A **sudden increase in discharge** without a corresponding pond rise.

## 7.5 Instruments and methods

- **Weirs and flumes** for total seepage quantity.
- **Piezometers and observation wells** for phreatic surface.
- **Visual surveillance** for location, clarity, and turbidity ([Chapter 3](chapter-03-philosophy.md)).
- **Decant monitoring** to confirm pond level and freeboard.

## 7.6 Key takeaways

- Quantify seepage and watch for change, especially turbidity.
- Keep the phreatic surface low and within the beach; protect freeboard.
- Beach geometry is a safety parameter, not just operations.
- Piping announces itself through new, turbid, or increasing seepage.
