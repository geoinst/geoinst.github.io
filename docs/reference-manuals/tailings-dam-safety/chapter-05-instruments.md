# Chapter 5 — Instrument Families for Tailings

## 5.1 The toolkit at a glance

Tailings surveillance draws on four instrument families. The links below point to
the [Dunnicliff](../dunnicliff/index.md) and [FHWA](../fhwa.md) manuals for detailed
installation procedures.

| Family | What it measures | Representative instruments |
|--------|-----------------|----------------------------|
| **Survey** | Surface position, elevation, movement | Total stations, GNSS, levelling, drone photogrammetry |
| **Geotechnical** | Pore pressure, internal deformation | Piezometers, inclinometers, extensometers, tiltmeters |
| **Hydrologic** | Pond level, seepage quantity | Staff gauges, weirs, flow meters, rainfall |
| **Remote sensing** | Wide-area, mm-scale deformation | Satellite InSAR, aerial LiDAR, thermal imaging |

## 5.2 Survey instruments

Survey monuments on the crest, beach, and toe reveal settlement, lateral
movement, and slumping. Modern programs combine:

- **Robotic total stations** for automated, round-the-clock monitoring of
  prisms.
- **GNSS** for absolute positioning where line-of-sight is difficult.
- **Drone (UAV) photogrammetry** for rapid, repeatable surface models and
  beach-geometry mapping.

## 5.3 Geotechnical instruments

- **Piezometers** ([Chapter 6](chapter-06-pore-pressure.md)) measure pore
  pressure — the single most important parameter for liquefaction and piping.
- **Inclinometers and extensometers** ([Chapter 8](chapter-08-deformation.md))
  detect internal shear and settlement.
- **Tiltmeters** sense rotation of crest or structure.

## 5.4 Hydrologic instruments

Pond level, freeboard, and seepage discharge are simple but vital. Staff gauges,
bubbler or pressure-level sensors, V-notch weirs, and tipping-bucket rain gauges
quantify the water balance that drives most failure modes.

## 5.5 Remote sensing (InSAR and beyond)

Satellite radar interferometry (InSAR / A-DInSAR) measures ground displacement
over the whole facility and surrounding terrain at millimeter precision, with no
instruments on site. It is especially valuable for detecting **regional or
unexpected** movement and for facilities in remote areas. It complements — but
does not replace — point instruments and inspection.

!!! warning "No single instrument is enough"
    Redundancy matters for high-consequence facilities. Combine independent
    methods (e.g., pore pressure by both vibrating-wire and standpipe, movement by
    both survey and InSAR) so a single failure or gap does not create a blind spot.

## 5.6 Selecting instruments

Selection weighs accuracy, range, durability, power, telemetry, and cost against
the failure mode and the decision the data supports. The detailed trade-offs live
in the [Dunnicliff](../dunnicliff/index.md) instrument chapters and
[FHWA](../fhwa.md) manual.

## 5.7 Key takeaways

- Four families: survey, geotechnical, hydrologic, remote sensing.
- Pore pressure and deformation are the geotechnical priorities.
- Satellite InSAR adds whole-facility coverage with no on-site sensors.
- Combine independent methods for redundancy on high-consequence facilities.
