# Chapter 12 — Data Evaluation and Interpretation

## 12.1 Evaluation turns data into understanding

Collecting data is not monitoring; **evaluating** it is. Evaluation asks whether
measured behavior matches expected behavior ([Chapter 1](chapter-01-introduction.md))
and what a deviation means.

## 12.2 Baselines and expected behavior

Expected behavior is established by:

- **Design analyses** (stability, seepage, deformation).
- **The early-operation baseline** — the first years of readings under known
  conditions.
- **Empirical norms** for similar facilities and instruments.

Deviations are judged against these, not against arbitrary numbers.

## 12.3 Plots that reveal signal

- **Time-series** of each parameter against pond level, rainfall, and season.
- **Cross-plots** (e.g., crest settlement vs. piezometric head).
- **Profiles** (pore pressure with depth; deformation with distance).
- **Cumulative vs. rate plots** to distinguish steady from accelerating movement.

## 12.4 Separating signal from noise

All instruments have noise. The interpreter must distinguish:

- **Real trend** (monotonic change with a physical cause).
- **Seasonal/operational variation** (pond cycle, temperature, deposition).
- **Instrument artifact** (drift, clogging, electrical fault).

Correlation with independent parameters (does movement follow pore pressure?)
is the strongest test of a real signal.

## 12.5 The role of satellite InSAR in evaluation

Wide-area displacement maps from InSAR reveal movement that point instruments
miss — and confirm that point instruments are representative. Anomalous InSAR
zones warrant targeted ground investigation.

!!! tip "Rate of change beats magnitude"
    Acceleration — a change in the rate of movement or pore-pressure rise — is
    often the earliest and most reliable warning. Watch derivatives, not just
    values.

## 12.6 Key takeaways

- Evaluation compares measured to expected behavior.
- Build baselines from design, early operation, and norms.
- Use time-series, cross-plots, and profiles to find signal.
- Watch rates of change; acceleration is the earliest warning.
