# Chapter 10 — Installation, Operation, and Maintenance

## 10.1 Reliability is designed in

A monitoring program is only as good as the data it actually collects. Most
failures of surveillance are not sensor errors but **installation and
maintenance** failures. The [Dunnicliff](../dunnicliff/index.md) "recipe for
reliability" applies directly to tailings facilities.

## 10.2 Installation essentials

- **Protect against the environment** — tailings sites are corrosive, sunny,
  dusty, and sometimes frozen. Use UV-stable cable, sealed connectors, and
  rodent/cable protection.
- **Proper saturation and grouting** of piezometers and inclinometer casing
  ([Dunnicliff Ch. 9, 17](../dunnicliff/index.md)).
- **Stable monuments** anchored below the active zone, with clear referencing.
- **Installation records** — depth, stratum, orientation, date, as-built — kept
  permanently.

## 10.3 Operation

- Establish **baseline readings** after installation and settlement.
- Set **reading frequencies** by parameter and risk stage ([Chapter 11](chapter-11-data-management.md)).
- Use **redundant manual backup** where automation is the primary method, so a
  telemetry failure does not create a gap.

## 10.4 Maintenance

- **Scheduled checks**: cable continuity, battery/Power, sensor zero, datalogger
  clock, telemetry heartbeat.
- **Cold-weather plans**: heating, burial depth, or seasonal shutdown with
  documented fallback.
- **Spare parts and tools** on site, with trained personnel.
- **Corrective action** when an instrument drifts, fails, or is destroyed by
  operations (e.g., buried by deposition).

## 10.5 Surviving the operating environment

Tailings operations are dynamic: deposition moves, equipment traverses, and
instruments get buried or struck. The plan must anticipate **instrument loss and
replacement** as a normal cost of monitoring a living facility.

!!! tip "Document everything"
    An instrument without a readable, permanent installation record and a known
    baseline is nearly worthless for interpretation. Treat records as first-class
    deliverables.

## 10.6 Key takeaways

- Most surveillance failures are installation/maintenance failures, not sensor errors.
- Protect instruments from a harsh, dynamic operating environment.
- Keep baselines, frequencies, and permanent records.
- Plan for instrument loss and replacement as normal.
