# Chapter 8 — Deformation Monitoring: Survey and Geotechnical

## 8.1 Movement is the bottom line

Ultimately, a tailings failure is **movement** — of the crest, the beach, or the
deposit. Deformation monitoring detects movement before it becomes a failure.

## 8.2 Surface deformation

- **Survey monuments** on crest, beach, and toe measured by total station, GNSS,
  or leveling reveal settlement and lateral displacement.
- **Robotic total stations** track prisms automatically and can alarm on
  threshold exceedance.
- **Drone photogrammetry** builds repeatable surface models to map broad,
  low-rate movement and beach change.

## 8.3 Internal deformation

Some of the most dangerous movement is **internal** — a sliding surface within
the deposit or foundation that no surface monument reveals.

- **Inclinometers** (probe or in-place) detect lateral shear and the depth of a
  shear plane.
- **Extensometers** (rod, magnetic, wire) measure settlement or heave at depth.
- **Multi-point borehole extensometers (MPBX)** combine several depths in one
  borehole.
- **Tiltmeters** sense rotation of crest or structure.

## 8.4 Choosing depth vs. surface

Surface survey catches gross movement; geotechnical instruments catch the
**internal shear and settlement** that precedes it. For high-consequence
facilities, use both, with geotechnical instruments placed along credible shear
surfaces identified in the failure-mode analysis ([Chapter 2](chapter-02-facilities-and-failures.md)).

## 8.5 Frequency and automation

During raises, storms, or seismic events, movement can accelerate within hours.
Automated survey and in-place inclinometers provide the temporal resolution that
manual surveys cannot. Pair them with trigger levels ([Chapter 13](chapter-13-decisions.md)).

!!! tip "Correlate movement with pore pressure"
    The most informative interpretation correlates deformation with pore
    pressure: movement under rising $r_u$ ([Chapter 6](chapter-06-pore-pressure.md))
    is far more alarming than movement under stable pore pressure.

## 8.6 Common pitfalls

- Monuments placed only on the crest miss beach and toe movement.
- Inclinometer casing not grouted properly reads the wrong zone.
- Manual surveys too infrequent to catch rapid change.

## 8.7 Key takeaways

- Movement — surface and internal — is the ultimate failure signal.
- Use both surface survey and internal geotechnical instruments.
- Correlate deformation with pore pressure for context.
- Automate where movement can accelerate quickly.
