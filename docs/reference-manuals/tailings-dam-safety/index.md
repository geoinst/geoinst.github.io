# Tailings Dam Safety: An Engineering Reference

!!! note "About this reference"
    This is an **original, open technical reference** on the monitoring and safe
    management of tailings storage facilities (TSFs) and their dams, written in
    plain engineering language. It is adapted from the subject scope of **ICOLD
    Bulletin 194 — *Tailings Dams: Safety, Monitoring and Surveillance* (2021)**
    and related ICOLD / mining-industry guidance. It is **not** a reproduction of
    the copyrighted source text. It is provided for education and field use. Every
    tailings facility is unique and carries high consequence of failure; this
    reference is meant to build engineering judgment, not to set minimum
    standards. Always follow the applicable regulatory regime and the facility's
    own surveillance plan.

Tailings are the finely ground waste solids left after extracting minerals from
ore. They are mixed with water and deposited behind engineered embankments —
**tailings dams** — that are often among the largest man-made structures on Earth.
Because they are built from the waste product itself, and raised repeatedly over
decades, they demand a distinct and disciplined approach to safety, monitoring,
and surveillance.

This reference walks through that approach: understanding how tailings facilities
fail, building a risk-informed monitoring program, selecting and maintaining
instruments, and turning measurements into defensible decisions.

## A note on units and terminology

Both SI and US customary units appear in practice; this reference uses the unit
set most common to each topic and states conversions where they matter. A
**tailings storage facility (TSF)** includes the impoundment, embankment(s),
deposition system, decant, and reclaim works. **Surveillance** here means the
combination of human observation and instrument measurement used to confirm a
facility is performing as expected.

> **Safety first.** Instrument data supports, but never replaces, qualified
> visual inspection and the engineering judgment of a qualified tailings engineer
> and the facility's dam safety program.
