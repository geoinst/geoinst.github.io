# Chapter 8 — Measurement of Deformation

## 8.1 Deformation is the visible consequence

Most geotechnical failures announce themselves as **movement**. Deformation
monitoring detects it — at the surface and, critically, deep underground where the
eye cannot see.

## 8.2 Extensometers — measuring movement at depth

**Extensometers** measure relative displacement between an anchor and a
reference, revealing settlement, heave, or separation at depth:

- **Rod extensometers** — rigid rods to anchors; simple, robust.
- **Magnetic extensometers** — magnetic rings on a access tube; multiple depths.
- **Wire (sonic) extensometers** — remote reading; good for long intervals.
- **Multi-point borehole (MPBX)** — several anchors in one borehole.

## 8.3 Inclinometers — detecting shear

**Inclinometers** measure lateral displacement versus depth, revealing the
location and magnitude of a shear plane:

- **Probe (traversing) inclinometers** — a probe lowered down a casing.
- **In-place (fixed) inclinometers** — sensors fixed in the casing for automated
  monitoring.

Casing installation (verticality, grouting) determines data quality.

## 8.4 Tiltmeters, settlement, convergence, crackmeters

- **Tiltmeters** — rotation of a surface or structure.
- **Settlement systems** — liquid, magnetic, or tape; measure vertical movement
  over areas (e.g., embankments, foundations).
- **Convergence** — shortening between points in a tunnel or excavation.
- **Crackmeters** — opening of joints, cracks, or joints in structures.

## 8.5 Choosing the right tool

The choice depends on whether movement is vertical or lateral, surface or
internal, slow or fast, and whether automation is needed. Often several are
combined: an MPBX plus an inclinometer in the same borehole is a powerful
combination.

!!! tip "Depth matters"
    Surface survey alone misses internal shear. Where a deep failure surface is
    credible, pair surface monitoring with borehole instruments along that
    surface.

## 8.6 Key takeaways

- Deformation is the usual pre-failure signal; measure it surface and deep.
- Extensometers reveal movement at depth; inclinometers locate shear planes.
- Tilt, settlement, convergence, and crackmeters cover the remaining modes.
- Combine instruments to catch both surface and internal movement.
