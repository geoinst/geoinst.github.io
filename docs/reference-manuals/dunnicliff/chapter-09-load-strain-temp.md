# Chapter 9 — Measurement of Load, Strain & Temperature

## 9.1 Monitoring forces in structures

Where geotechnical works interact with structures — struts, anchors, piles,
linings — monitoring the **load and strain** in those members confirms they
behave as designed and reveals distress early.

## 9.2 Load cells

**Load cells** measure force. Common types:

- **Vibrating-wire** — automated, widely used in struts, anchors, and piles.
- **Hydraulic** — robust, good for high forces.
- **Electrical (strain-gage)** — versatile, sensitive to lead effects.

Applications include braced-excavation strut loads, anchor prestress, and pile
load tests.

## 9.3 Strain gages

**Strain gages** measure deformation per unit length, from which stress can be
inferred:

- **Bonded wire / foil** — surface-mounted on steel or concrete.
- **Vibrating-wire** — embeddable in concrete or attached to steel.
- **Telltales** — simple devices indicating relative movement across a section.

## 9.4 Temperature measurement

Temperature affects nearly every other instrument: it changes sensor output,
concrete strain, and pore pressure. **Thermistors, RTDs, thermocouples, and
mercury thermometers** are used to:

- Compensate other readings for thermal effects.
- Detect freezing, hydration heat, or thermal-driven movement.

## 9.5 Interpretation cautions

Strain in concrete includes shrinkage and creep; separate these from load
effects using temperature and companion specimens. Load-cell readings must be
referenced to a verified zero.

!!! warning "Temperature is not an afterthought"
    Ignoring temperature compensation is a leading cause of misinterpreted
    strain and pore-pressure data. Measure temperature wherever thermal effects
    are plausible.

## 9.6 Key takeaways

- Load cells and strain gages confirm structural members behave as designed.
- VW, hydraulic, and electrical types serve different needs.
- Temperature affects almost every reading — measure and compensate it.
- Separate shrinkage/creep from load effects in concrete.
