# Chapter 1 — Introduction: An Overview

## 1.1 What geotechnical instrumentation is

Geotechnical instrumentation is the measurement of parameters that reveal how
soil, rock, foundations, and structures behave under load. It converts the
invisible — internal pore pressure, deep deformation, hidden stress — into
numbers that engineers can act on.

Typical measured parameters include:

- Groundwater (pore) pressure
- Total and effective stress
- Deformation (settlement, heave, lateral movement)
- Load and strain in structural members
- Temperature (which affects nearly every other reading)

## 1.2 Why it is needed

We cannot see underground. A slope may be sliding on a deep shear plane, a
foundation may be settling differentially, a tunnel may be converging — none
visible from the surface. Instrumentation makes the hidden observable and,
crucially, **measurable over time**, so trends are caught early.

## 1.3 The chain of success

Dunnicliff frames monitoring as a **chain with 25 links**, from the moment a
project's need is defined to the moment a reading informs a decision. Break any
link — poor planning, wrong instrument, bad installation, unread data — and the
whole effort fails. This book is, in effect, a tour of those links.

## 1.4 Behavior of soil and rock (brief)

Instrument selection depends on how soil and rock actually respond to load:

- **Soil** deforms, consolidates, and can lose strength when pore pressure rises.
- **Rock** behaves via joints and discontinuities more than via intact strength.
- **Interfaces** (soil–structure, rock–support) are where movement often
  concentrates.

Understanding these fundamentals is prerequisite to choosing what to measure.

## 1.5 Scope of this reference

This book covers the planning, instruments, execution, and interpretation of
geotechnical monitoring for slopes, embankments, dams, tunnels, foundations, and
excavations. It is original, educational content adapted from Dunnicliff's scope;
it is not a reproduction of the copyrighted text.

## 1.6 Key takeaways

- Instrumentation makes the hidden subsurface observable and measurable.
- It is a chain of 25 links; failure at any link defeats the purpose.
- Soil and rock behave differently; selection follows behavior.
- Judgment, not hardware alone, produces useful monitoring.
