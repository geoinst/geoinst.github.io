# Chapter 4 — Procurement Specifications & Contractual Arrangements

## 4.1 Buying instruments is buying performance

A vague specification produces vague, unreliable data. The procurement documents
must state exactly what is required of the instrument system and its
installation.

## 4.2 Specifications for procurement

A good specification covers:

- **Performance** — range, accuracy, resolution, operating conditions.
- **Factory calibration** — required certificates, traceability, and as-delivered
  calibration values.
- **Spare parts and consumables** — sensors, cables, readout units.
- **Documentation** — installation manuals, datasheets, software.
- **Acceptance testing** — how the delivered system is verified before
  installation.

## 4.3 Factory vs. field calibration

Factory calibration establishes the sensor's relationship between input and
output. Field conditions (cable length, temperature, interference) can shift
that relationship, so field verification and, where needed, field calibration
are part of a trustworthy program.

## 4.4 Contractual arrangements

Field instrumentation services can be delivered through several contract
structures:

- **Separate specialty contract** — the instrument supplier installs and reads.
- **Assigned subcontract** — the general contractor sub-lets instrumentation.
- **Bid items within the works** — instrumentation priced as line items.
- **Prequalification** — requiring demonstrated competence for sensitive work.

The choice affects accountability, coordination, and data quality. Critically,
the party *reading and interpreting* the data should be technically competent and
independent enough to report bad news.

## 4.5 Qualifications matter

Installation and reading of geotechnical instruments are skilled work. Specify
personnel qualifications and require installation records (depth, orientation,
stratum, as-built) as deliverables.

!!! tip "Specify the data, not just the device"
    A specification that demands "a vibrating-wire piezometer" but not "a
    validated weekly pore-pressure time series with stated accuracy" may get the
    device and miss the data. Specify outcomes.

## 4.6 Key takeaways

- Specify performance, calibration, spares, and acceptance — not just a device.
- Verify calibration in the field, not only at the factory.
- Choose a contract structure that preserves accountability and competence.
- Require qualified people and installation records as deliverables.
