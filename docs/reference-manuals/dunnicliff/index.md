# Geotechnical Instrumentation for Monitoring Field Performance

!!! note "About this reference"
    This is an **original, open technical reference** on geotechnical
    instrumentation, written in plain engineering language and adapted from the
    subject scope and structure of **John Dunnicliff's *Geotechnical
    Instrumentation for Monitoring Field Performance*** — the definitive reference
    textbook used worldwide for planning monitoring programs — together with the
    complementary **NCHRP Synthesis 89** of the same name. It is **not** a
    reproduction of the copyrighted text. It is provided for education and field
    use, and every project is unique: the guidance here is meant to build
    engineering judgment, not to set minimum standards. The full Dunnicliff text
    and NCHRP Synthesis 89 are indexed in the [GTI Doctor](../../gti-doctor.md)
    AI assistant for deeper queries.

**Geotechnical instrumentation** is the discipline of measuring how soil, rock,
foundations, and structures actually behave under real loads. It turns "we hope
it's fine" into "we know it's fine" — or, more importantly, "we can see it
moving." This reference walks through the full arc of a monitoring program: why
instrument, how to plan it systematically, which instruments measure which
parameters, how to install and maintain them reliably, and how to turn readings
into decisions.

## A note on terminology

"Monitoring" here means the measurement of geotechnical parameters to confirm
that behavior matches expectation. An **instrument** includes the sensor, its
installation, the readout or datalogger, and the people and procedures that turn
a reading into a decision. All three are necessary — a good sensor badly
installed and unread is worthless.

> **Judgment is the instrument.** Instruments support, but never replace, the
> engineering judgment of a qualified geotechnical engineer.
