# Chapter 1 — Introduction and Sources

## 1.1 What foundation engineering is

**Foundation engineering** is the branch of geotechnical engineering concerned with
the **interface between a structure and the ground**. It answers two questions
together:

1. **Can the ground carry the load?** — the *bearing capacity* problem.
2. **Will it move too much?** — the *settlement* and *deformation* problem.

It also designs the structures that hold earth back — retaining walls, sheet pile
walls, and braced excavations — and the analyses that keep slopes stable. Every
foundation design must satisfy **strength** (no bearing failure, no sliding, no
overturning) and **serviceability** (movement within tolerable limits) at the same
time.

## 1.2 Why this reference is built on public-domain sources

Most foundation engineering textbooks — including the well-known academic texts —
are **copyrighted**. Reproducing or closely paraphrasing them raises legal risk,
especially for a site that translates its content into another language and
publishes it openly.

The **USACE Engineering Manuals** and the **FHWA NHI geotechnical manuals** solve
this problem elegantly:

- They are **works of the U.S. federal government** and are therefore **not subject
  to copyright** in the United States — they may be reused, adapted, and
  translated freely.
- They are written for **practising engineers**, not students — they are directly
  design-oriented.
- They are **authoritative and current**: USACE and FHWA guidance is the basis for
  real infrastructure design across the United States and is widely referenced
  internationally.

## 1.3 The design process

Foundation design follows a consistent arc, whichever manual you follow:

1. **Investigate the site** — establish the soil and rock profile, groundwater
   regime, and engineering properties ([Chapter 2](chapter-02-site-exploration.md)).
2. **Choose the foundation type** — shallow (spread footings, mats) or deep (piles,
   drilled shafts), based on loads, ground conditions, and tolerable movement.
3. **Check bearing capacity** ([Chapter 3](chapter-03-bearing-capacity.md)) and
   **settlement** ([Chapter 4](chapter-04-settlement.md)).
4. **Design the structural elements** — footings, walls, sheet piles — and check
   their stability.
5. **Plan construction and monitoring** — installation, quality control, and
   instrumentation ([Chapter 14](chapter-14-ground-improvement.md)).

## 1.4 Limit states and factors of safety

Design must satisfy both **ultimate limit states** (collapse: bearing failure,
sliding, structural failure) and **serviceability limit states** (excessive
settlement or deflection). Two philosophies appear across the manuals:

- **Allowable-stress design (ASD)** — compute an ultimate capacity and divide by a
  **factor of safety**.
- **Load and resistance factor design (LRFD)** — apply **load factors** to demands
  and **resistance factors** to capacities.

This reference presents the underlying mechanics in allowable-stress terms, since
that is the common language of the source manuals, and notes where LRFD changes the
framing.

## 1.5 Key takeaways

- Foundation engineering = **bearing capacity + settlement + earth retention +
  slope stability**.
- This book is built **entirely from public-domain USACE and FHWA manuals** — free
  to reuse, adapt, and translate.
- Design must satisfy **strength** *and* **serviceability**.
- The process is always: **investigate → select type → check capacity and
  settlement → design details → construct and monitor**.
