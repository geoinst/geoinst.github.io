# Chapter 14 — Ground Improvement & Foundation Instrumentation

## 14.1 Why improve the ground

When the ground cannot support the proposed foundation economically, it can be
**improved in place** rather than bypassed with deep foundations. Ground improvement
increases strength, reduces compressibility, controls groundwater, or mitigates
liquefaction. It is often the most economical solution for weak, loose, or
compressible soils.

## 14.2 Densification methods

- **Vibro-compaction and vibro-replacement (stone columns)** — vibratory probes
  densify loose sands and form columns of compacted stone in soft clays.
- **Dynamic compaction** — a heavy weight dropped in a grid densifies granular fill
  and loose soils.
- **Deep dynamic replacement** — stone or rubble columns formed by dropping.
- **Compaction grouting** — stiff grout injected to densify and lift.

## 14.3 Reinforcement and inclusion

- **Stone columns** — increase bearing capacity and reduce settlement; also act as
  drains.
- **Soil nailing** — steel nails installed into a slope or excavation face to
  reinforce it (FHWA GEC-7).
- **Reinforced soil (MSE)** — geosynthetic or metallic reinforcement in fill
  ([Chapter 11](chapter-11-retaining-walls.md)).
- **Micropiles** — small-diameter drilled and grouted piles for restricted access or
  underpinning.

## 14.4 Grouting and drainage

- **Permeation grouting** — fills voids in granular soils to reduce permeability and
  increase strength.
- **Jet grouting** — high-pressure grout creates soil–cement columns.
- **Drainage** — vertical drains, wick drains, and horizontal drains accelerate
  consolidation and lower pore pressures.

## 14.5 Instrumentation for foundations

Because ground improvement and deep foundations are expensive and difficult to
verify, **instrumentation** is essential to confirm performance:

- **Settlement platforms and extensometers** — measure settlement and its
  distribution.
- **Piezometers** — track pore pressures during consolidation and loading.
- **Inclinometers** — detect lateral movement in slopes and behind excavations.
- **Load cells and strain gages** — measure load in piles, anchors, and struts.
- **Settlement and heave points** — verify ground improvement and adjacent
  structures.

These instruments, and the practice of using them, are covered in depth in the
[Dunnicliff](../dunnicliff/index.md) and [FHWA](../fhwa/index.md) references.

## 14.6 Key takeaways

- **Ground improvement** is often cheaper than deep foundations for weak ground.
- **Densification** (vibro, dynamic compaction) treats loose and compressible soils.
- **Inclusion and reinforcement** (stone columns, soil nails, MSE, micropiles) add
  strength and stiffness.
- **Grouting and drainage** control permeability and pore pressure.
- **Instrument** every improvement and foundation — verification is essential
  because the ground is hidden.
