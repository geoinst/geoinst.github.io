# Chapter 9 — Drilled Shafts & Caissons

## 9.1 Drilled shafts

A **drilled shaft** (bored pile) is a cast-in-place reinforced-concrete element
constructed by drilling a hole and filling it with concrete. Diameters range from
about 0.5 m to 3 m or more, and lengths can exceed 60 m. Drilled shafts offer:

- **high axial and lateral capacity** from a single element;
- **low noise and vibration** — suited to urban sites;
- the ability to **inspect and instrument** the excavation before concreting;
- a **single element** that can replace a pile group, avoiding a pile cap.

## 9.2 Construction methods

The method depends on the ground and groundwater:

- **Dry method** — in stable, dry ground, the hole stands open.
- **Casing method** — a temporary or permanent steel casing supports the hole
  through caving soils or under water.
- **Slurry method** — a bentonite or polymer slurry stabilizes the hole, displaced by
  the concrete as it is placed by **tremie**.
- **Continuous Flight Auger (CFA)** — a hollow auger is drilled and withdrawn while
  concrete is pumped through it.

Each method affects capacity: the **slurry method** can leave a filter cake and
softened zone that reduces shaft friction if not cleaned.

## 9.3 Capacity

Drilled-shaft capacity follows the same **shaft + base** framework as piles, but with
different coefficients:

- **Shaft resistance** in clay is lower than for driven piles (no densification);
  in sand it depends on construction method and cleaning.
- **Base resistance** requires a **clean base**; debris or softened soil drastically
  reduces end bearing.
- **Base grouting** can be used to improve base contact and capacity.

## 9.4 Caissons

**Caissons** are deep foundation elements installed by excavation from within:

- **Open caissons** — a shaft sunk by excavating from the bottom.
- **Box caissons** — a prefabricated box floated to site and sunk onto prepared
  ground.
- **Pneumatic caissons** — a sealed working chamber under compressed air (rarely
  used today).

Caissons suit deep water, river beds, and situations where a large, monolithic
foundation is required.

## 9.5 Inspection and integrity

Because drilled shafts are formed in the ground, **integrity** must be verified:

- **Cross-hole sonic logging (CSL)** — ultrasonic probes in tubes cast into the
  shaft detect defects.
- **Low-strain integrity tests (PIT)** — a hammer tap and reflection analysis.
- **Core drilling** — direct sampling where defects are suspected.

## 9.6 Key takeaways

- **Drilled shafts** give high capacity in a single element, with low vibration and
  the ability to instrument before concreting.
- **Construction method** (dry, casing, slurry, CFA) controls capacity — cleaning is
  critical.
- **Base cleanliness** governs end bearing; base grouting can help.
- **Caissons** are excavated-in-place foundations for deep water and large
  monoliths.
- **Verify integrity** with CSL, PIT, or coring.
