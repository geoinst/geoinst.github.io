# Chapter 2 — Subsurface Exploration & Site Characterization

## 2.1 Why explore

Every foundation design is only as good as the ground model behind it. **Subsurface
exploration** establishes the stratigraphy, groundwater conditions, and engineering
properties needed to select a foundation type and compute capacity and settlement.
Under-exploration is the most common root cause of foundation problems.

## 2.2 The exploration programme

A programme is planned around the **structure and the questions** it raises:

- **Purpose** — what loads, what tolerable movement, what construction methods?
- **Layout** — boreholes and soundings positioned to cover the structure footprint
  and any variability; more closely spaced where conditions change.
- **Depth** — deep enough to pass through all layers that contribute significantly
  to settlement, or to reach competent bearing strata.
- **Methods** — a combination of **boreholes with sampling** and **in-situ tests**.

## 2.3 Sampling and in-situ testing

- **Boreholes** recover disturbed and undisturbed samples for laboratory testing.
- The **Standard Penetration Test (SPT)** gives the blow count $N$ — a widely used
  index of density and strength, and the basis of many empirical correlations.
- The **Cone Penetration Test (CPT)** gives continuous tip resistance and sleeve
  friction, excellent for profiling soft soils and estimating strength and
  consolidation parameters.
- **Vane shear**, **pressuremeter**, and **dilatometer** tests provide specific
  parameters in situ.

## 2.4 Groundwater

Groundwater governs effective stress and therefore strength and settlement.
Establish:

- the **depth to the water table** and its seasonal range;
- **pore pressures** and any artesian conditions;
- the **permeability** of each layer, for seepage and consolidation.

Pore pressures are measured with **piezometers** (see the
[Dunnicliff](../dunnicliff/index.md) and [FHWA](../fhwa/index.md) references).

## 2.5 From data to a design profile

Exploration produces a **design profile**: a simplified, conservative model of the
layers, their properties, and the groundwater regime, expressed as parameters for
the analyses in the following chapters. The profile should:

- **bound uncertainty** — adopt conservative values where data are sparse;
- **identify problem soils** — soft clays, loose sands, expansive or collapsible
  soils, organics, fill;
- **flag variability** — where properties change sharply across the site.

## 2.6 Key takeaways

- **Explore before you design** — the ground model drives every decision.
- Combine **boreholes** with **in-situ tests** (SPT, CPT) and **groundwater
  measurement**.
- Depth and spacing follow the **structure and its questions**.
- Reduce the data to a conservative, clearly-stated **design profile**.
- Watch for **problem soils** and **variability**.
