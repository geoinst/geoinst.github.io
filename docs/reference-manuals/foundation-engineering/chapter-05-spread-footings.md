# Chapter 5 — Spread Footings & Mats

## 5.1 Footing types

**Shallow foundations** distribute structural loads over a wide area at or near the
ground surface:

- **Isolated (pad) footings** — single columns.
- **Combined footings** — two or more columns sharing one footing.
- **Strip (wall) footings** — continuous under a wall.
- **Mat (raft) foundations** — a single slab under the whole structure, used where
  loads are large, soils are weak, or differential settlement must be minimized.

## 5.2 Proportioning for bearing and settlement

Footing size is set by the more demanding of:

- **bearing capacity** — the base pressure must not exceed the allowable bearing
  pressure ([Chapter 3](chapter-03-bearing-capacity.md)); and
- **settlement** — the computed settlement must not exceed tolerable movement
  ([Chapter 4](chapter-04-settlement.md)).

For eccentric loading, proportion the footing so the resultant lies within the
**middle third** where possible (no tension at the base), and use the **effective
area** method for capacity.

## 5.3 Depth of embedment

Footing depth is chosen to:

- reach a **competent bearing stratum** below weak or organic surface soils;
- be **below the frost depth** (frost heave) and below the **zone of seasonal volume
  change** in expansive clays;
- provide adequate **cover** and resistance to scour, erosion, and uplift;
- avoid disturbance from adjacent construction.

## 5.4 Mat foundations

A **mat** is used when:

- spread footings would be too large or would produce excessive differential
  settlement;
- the soil is weak and variable;
- loads are heavy and closely spaced;
- uplift or hydrostatic conditions favour a large, stiff base.

Mats spread load and equalize settlement, but they are analysed as a **soil–structure
interaction** problem, with the mat stiffness and the soil modulus both important.

## 5.5 Construction and quality control

- **Subgrade preparation** — proof-roll, compact, and protect the bearing surface;
  do not disturb or soften it.
- **Water control** — keep the excavation dry; water softening of the base is a
  common cause of settlement.
- **Backfill and drainage** — provide free-draining backfill and positive drainage
  away from the structure.
- **Concrete cover** — adequate cover for durability in aggressive ground.

## 5.6 Key takeaways

- Footings are sized by the **more demanding** of bearing capacity and settlement.
- Keep the resultant within the **middle third** where possible; use effective area
  for eccentricity.
- Set **embedment depth** for bearing stratum, frost, expansive soil, and scour.
- **Mats** equalize settlement but are a soil–structure interaction problem.
- **Protect the subgrade** — construction water and disturbance are common causes of
  poor performance.
