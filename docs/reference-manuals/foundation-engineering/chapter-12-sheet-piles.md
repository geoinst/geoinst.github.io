# Chapter 12 — Sheet Pile Walls & Braced Excavations

## 12.1 Sheet pile walls

A **sheet pile wall** is a continuous wall of interlocking steel (or concrete/timber)
sheets driven into the ground. It is used for:

- **excavation support** (temporary or permanent);
- **waterfront structures** and **cofferdams**;
- **cut-off walls** to control seepage.

Sheet piles resist earth and water pressure primarily by **bending**, and derive
stability from **embedment depth** below the excavation and, where used, from
**anchors** or **struts**.

## 12.2 Cantilever sheet pile walls

A **cantilever** wall has no support above the excavation and relies entirely on its
**embedment** for stability. It is economical only for **modest retained heights**
(commonly up to about 4–6 m, and less in weak soils). Design determines the
**required embedment depth** and the **maximum bending moment** from the pressure
distribution, applying a **factor of safety on the passive resistance** and often a
**simplified (e.g. "equivalent beam")** method.

## 12.3 Anchored sheet pile walls

For taller walls, a **tie-back anchor** or a **deadman** near the top reduces the
bending moment and required embedment. Design considers:

- **earth pressure** on the wall (active behind, passive in front);
- **anchor force** and its location;
- **free-earth support** (the wall rotates about the anchor) vs **fixed-earth
  support** (the wall is fixed below the excavation).

## 12.4 Braced (strutted) excavations

A **braced excavation** supports the walls with **struts** across the opening (or
**cross-lot bracing**), often with **wales** distributing load to the struts. Key
design and construction issues:

- **Apparent earth pressure** envelopes (e.g. **Tschebotarioff** or **Peck**) are
  used to size the bracing, since real strut loads differ from simple theory.
- **Preloading** the struts reduces wall movement and ground settlement.
- **Construction sequence** (excavate–install–preload) strongly influences
  movements.
- **Base stability** — heave, boiling, and piping must be checked, especially in soft
  clay and with high groundwater.

## 12.5 Movement, settlement, and monitoring

Excavation inevitably causes some ground movement and settlement of adjacent
structures. Managing it requires:

- **stiff support** and **preloading**;
- **sequential excavation** in stages;
- **monitoring** — inclinometers in the wall and ground, load cells on struts,
  settlement points on adjacent structures, and piezometers (see the
  [FHWA](../fhwa/index.md) reference, Chapter 9).

## 12.6 Key takeaways

- **Sheet pile walls** resist pressure by bending; stability comes from
  **embedment**, and from **anchors or struts** for taller walls.
- **Cantilever** walls suit modest heights; **anchored/braced** walls suit taller
  ones.
- Size bracing with **apparent earth pressure** envelopes; **preload** struts to
  limit movement.
- Check **base stability** (heave, boiling, piping).
- **Monitor** movement and settlement of adjacent ground and structures.
