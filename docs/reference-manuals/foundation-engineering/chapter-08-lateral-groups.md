# Chapter 8 — Deep Foundations: Lateral Loads, Groups & Settlement

## 8.1 Laterally loaded piles

Piles resist **lateral loads** and **moments** from wind, seismic action, earth
pressure, water, and eccentric vertical loads. A laterally loaded pile behaves as a
**beam on an elastic foundation**, with the soil providing a stiffness that varies
with depth. The key parameters are:

- the pile's **flexural stiffness** $EI$;
- the soil's **modulus of subgrade reaction** (or $p$–$y$ curves);
- the **fixity** at the pile head (free or fixed).

Short, stiff piles fail by **soil failure**; long, slender piles fail by **structural
yielding**. The **characteristic length** distinguishes the two.

## 8.2 Pile groups

Piles are usually used in **groups**. Group behaviour differs from single piles:

- **Group efficiency** — the capacity of a group is not simply the sum of single-pile
  capacities; overlapping stress zones can reduce efficiency, especially in clay.
- **Block failure** — a closely spaced group may fail as a single block.
- **Settlement amplification** — group settlement exceeds single-pile settlement
  because the stress influence zones overlap and extend deeper.

Design checks both **single-pile** and **group (block)** capacity and settlement.

## 8.3 Negative skin friction (downdrag)

**Negative skin friction** occurs when the soil around a pile settles *more* than the
pile — for example, a pile driven through a consolidating fill or a soft clay into a
firm stratum. The settling soil drags *down* on the shaft, adding load rather than
resisting it. The result is **downdrag**, which must be added to the design load and
can be substantial. Mitigation includes **bitumen coating** or a **sleeve** on the
upper shaft.

## 8.4 Settlement of piles

Pile settlement has two parts:

- **Elastic shortening** of the pile under load; and
- **Settlement of the soil** at the base and along the shaft, from the load
  transferred to it.

Settlement is estimated from **elastic theory**, **load-transfer ($t$–$z$) analyses**,
or **empirical correlations**, and is confirmed by the **load test**. Group
settlement is larger and extends deeper than single-pile settlement.

## 8.5 Uplift and tension

Piles may be required to resist **uplift** (tension) from wind, seismic, or
hydrostatic forces. Uplift capacity comes from **shaft friction** and, for some
types, from an **enlarged base** or **anchor**. The available shaft resistance in
tension is generally lower than in compression.

## 8.6 Key takeaways

- Laterally loaded piles act as **beams on elastic foundations**; short piles fail in
  the soil, long piles yield structurally.
- **Group capacity ≠ sum of single piles** — check block failure and settlement
  amplification.
- **Negative skin friction (downdrag)** adds load where the soil settles more than
  the pile; mitigate with coatings or sleeves.
- Pile settlement = **elastic shortening + soil settlement**; groups settle more.
- **Uplift** capacity relies on shaft friction, generally lower than in compression.
