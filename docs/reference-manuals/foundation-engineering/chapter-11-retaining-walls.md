# Chapter 11 — Retaining Walls & MSE Walls

## 11.1 Wall types

- **Gravity walls** — mass concrete or stone; resist pressure by their own weight.
- **Cantilever walls** — a reinforced-concrete stem on a base; the most common
  type.
- **Counterfort and buttress walls** — cantilever walls with ribs for tall heights.
- **Mechanically stabilized earth (MSE) walls** — a facing panel plus
  **soil reinforcement** (strips, grids, or geotextiles) in the backfill.
- **Sheet pile and bored-pile walls** — covered in
  [Chapter 12](chapter-12-sheet-piles.md).

## 11.2 Stability checks

Every retaining wall is checked for three failure modes:

1. **Overturning** about the toe — the resisting moment (weight, passive pressure)
   must exceed the overturning moment from the lateral thrust.
2. **Sliding** along the base — the resisting force (base friction plus passive
   pressure) must exceed the driving lateral force.
3. **Bearing failure** of the foundation soil — the eccentric base pressure must not
   exceed the allowable bearing pressure, and should keep the resultant within the
   **middle third** where possible.

A global **slope stability** check ([Chapter 13](chapter-13-slope-stability.md)) is
also required, since the wall and its retained soil can fail as a mass.

## 11.3 Drainage and backfill

**Drainage is the single most important detail.** A wall must never be designed to
resist full hydrostatic pressure unless it truly cannot drain:

- provide a **weep hole system** and/or a **drainage blanket** behind the wall;
- use **free-draining granular backfill**;
- **filter** the drainage to prevent clogging.

Backfill is placed and compacted in lifts, with compaction controlled to avoid
locking in excessive lateral pressure ([Chapter 10](chapter-10-lateral-earth-pressure.md)).

## 11.4 Mechanically stabilized earth (MSE) walls

MSE walls carry the lateral thrust into **soil reinforcement** that extends into the
retained fill. Key design aspects:

- **Internal stability** — the reinforcement must not break or pull out; tension is
  highest at the facing and decreases with depth.
- **External stability** — the reinforced soil mass as a whole must not slide,
  overturn, or overstress the foundation.
- **Facing** — panels, blocks, or wrapped geotextile.
- **Corrosion and durability** — metallic reinforcement must be protected against
  corrosion; design life governs the thickness.

MSE walls are economical, tolerate settlement, and are extensively covered in the
FHWA GEC-11 manual.

## 11.5 Key takeaways

- Check every wall for **overturning, sliding, and bearing** — plus **global
  stability**.
- **Drainage** is the most important design detail — never trap water behind a wall.
- **MSE walls** transfer thrust into soil reinforcement; check **internal and
  external** stability and **durability**.
- Keep the base resultant within the **middle third** where possible.
