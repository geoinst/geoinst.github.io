# Chapter 6 — Deep Foundations: Types & Load Transfer

## 6.1 When deep foundations are used

**Deep foundations** carry load to competent strata far below the surface, or
develop capacity along their length through friction. They are used when:

- surface soils are too weak or compressible for shallow foundations;
- loads are very large;
- scour, uplift, or lateral loads must be resisted;
- settlement must be tightly controlled;
- expansive or collapsible soils make shallow foundations unreliable.

## 6.2 Pile classification

Piles are classified by **material**, **installation method**, and **load-transfer
mechanism**:

- **By material:** timber, steel (H-piles, pipe piles, sheet piles), concrete
  (precast or cast-in-place), and composite.
- **By installation:** **driven** (displacement piles, which densify the soil),
  **drilled/bored** (non-displacement, which remove soil), and **jetted** or
  **vibrated**.
- **By load transfer:** **end-bearing** (tip rests on a hard stratum), **friction**
  (load carried by skin friction along the shaft), and **combined**.

## 6.3 How a pile carries load

A loaded pile mobilizes resistance in two places:

- **End bearing** at the toe, where the pile bears on or penetrates a stronger
  stratum; and
- **Skin friction** along the shaft, from the shear resistance between the pile and
  the soil.

The load is not uniform: it decreases with depth as the soil takes it up. At working
load, both components mobilize, but their relative shares depend on the soil profile
and the pile stiffness.

## 6.4 Installation effects

Installation changes the soil around the pile:

- **Driving** displaces and densifies soil, increasing the effective stress and
  capacity in sands, but can cause heave and damage to adjacent structures.
- **Drilling** relieves stress and may leave a smear zone, reducing capacity, but
  causes less disturbance.
- **Set-up (freeze)** — driven piles in clay gain capacity with time as excess pore
  pressures dissipate.

These effects mean **capacity is not a pure property of the soil** — it depends on
how the pile was installed.

## 6.5 Key takeaways

- Deep foundations are used for **weak surface soils, large loads, scour, uplift,
  lateral loads, and settlement control**.
- Classify piles by **material, installation, and load transfer**.
- A pile mobilizes **end bearing + skin friction**; load decreases with depth.
- **Installation changes the soil** — driving densifies, drilling relieves; set-up
  increases capacity over time.
- **Instrument** load tests to see the true distribution
  ([Chapter 7](chapter-07-axial-capacity.md)).
