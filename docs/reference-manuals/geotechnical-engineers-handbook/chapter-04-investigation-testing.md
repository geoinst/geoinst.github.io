# Chapter 4 — Investigation, Exploration and Testing

Chapter IV is the largest in the handbook and the one closest to this site's focus. It
runs from how to *classify and describe* a soil or rock in the field, through the stages
of an investigation campaign, to the drilling, sampling, geophysics and the full set of
in-situ and laboratory tests that produce design parameters.

## 4.1 Classifying and describing soil and rock

### 4.1.1 Soil classification for construction

For construction, soil is treated as a **construction material** made of a mixture of
grain fractions, which may be excavated and moved without blasting. The purpose of
classification is to **quantify** the properties of each soil type so that design and
construction decisions can be made consistently.

A classification system is used **together with empirical design methods**: the
engineer classifies the soil, then applies the empirical correlations accumulated by
other engineers, instead of relying on their own limited experience. Good classification
also lets a designer judge which tests are relevant and which parameters are
representative.

### 4.1.2 Recognising and describing soil

Field identification by visual and manual means — the basis of every borehole log.

### 4.1.3 Rock-mass classification for construction

Rock is classified as a **mass** (khối đá), not as a specimen: degree of weathering,
spacing and orientation of discontinuities, and intact strength all enter.

### 4.1.4 Recognising and describing rock

Field description of rock core and exposures.

## 4.2 The investigation campaign and its stages

### 4.2.1 Purpose and scope

The purpose and the target of the investigation must be defined before any borehole is
drilled.

### 4.2.2 Stages of the investigation

Investigation proceeds in **stages** matched to the design stage — preliminary,
detailed design, and construction — with the scope, spacing and depth of boreholes
increasing at each stage.

## 4.4 Test methods

### 4.4.1 In-situ tests

The handbook presents the standard in-situ tests — **SPT** (xuyên tiêu chuẩn),
**CPT** (xuyên tĩnh), **PMT / Menard pressuremeter** (nén ngang), **vane shear**
(cắt cánh) and plate loading — with their equipment, procedure and interpretation.

### 4.4.2 Laboratory tests

Index tests, consolidation, shear strength (direct shear, triaxial), compaction
(Proctor), CBR and chemical tests.

## 4.8 Key takeaways

- Classification is used **with empirical design methods**, not in isolation.
- Rock is classified as a **mass**, not a specimen.
- Investigation proceeds in **stages** matched to design stages.
- The in-situ test family — **SPT, CPT, PMT, vane, plate load** — supplies the parameters
  used in Chapters 5–6.
- **Corrosivity** of the ground matters for both buried instruments and permanent works.
