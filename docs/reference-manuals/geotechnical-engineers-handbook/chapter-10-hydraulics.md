# Chapter 10 — Engineering Hydraulics

Chapter X covers the hydraulics of soil: how water moves through the ground and what
that does to stability. It covers the states of water in soil, Darcy's law, permeability,
two-dimensional flow, flow nets, seepage forces, and their application to cut-off walls,
excavations and earth dams.

## 10.1 Free-water movement

### 10.1.1 States of water in the ground

The handbook distinguishes three states:

- **Formation water** (nước thành tạo) — water held in the chemical structure of the
  mineral particles.
- **Bound water** (nước liên kết) — water adsorbed as a film on the particle surfaces.
- **Pore water** (nước lỗ rỗng) — water in the voids, of which there are two kinds:
  **capillary water** (nước mao dẫn) and **free water** (nước tự do).

Free water below the water table obeys the laws of fluid flow; capillary water is held by
surface tension and is in equilibrium between the weight of the water column and the
upward suction.

### 10.1.2 Straight-line flow — Darcy's law

For laminar flow through soil, the discharge velocity is proportional to the hydraulic
gradient:

$$v = k\,i$$

where $k$ is the coefficient of permeability and $i$ is the hydraulic gradient.

## 10.2 Two-dimensional flow in a homogeneous isotropic medium

### 10.2.1 The flow net

A **flow net** (mạng dòng chảy) is a graphical solution of Laplace's equation: a family
of flow lines crossing a family of equipotential lines at right angles.

### 10.2.3 Seepage force (lực đẩy của dòng chảy)

Water flowing through soil exerts a **seepage force** on the soil skeleton in the
direction of flow. Where flow is upward, the force reduces effective stress and can lead
to **piping** or **boiling**.

## 10.4 Key takeaways

- Water in soil exists as **formation, bound and pore water**; pore water is capillary or
  free.
- **Darcy's law** $v = ki$ governs laminar seepage.
- A **flow net** solves 2-D flow graphically.
- **Seepage force** acts on the soil skeleton; upward flow reduces effective stress and
  can cause **piping**.
- The same theory applies to **cut-off walls, excavations and earth dams**.
