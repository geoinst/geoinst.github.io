# Chapter 6 — Deep Foundation Analysis

Chapter VI treats the pile: how piles are classified, how a single pile carries load,
what happens when the ground moves against the pile, how piles behave under lateral
load and in groups, and how the group settles.

## 6.1 Basic concepts

### 6.1.1 Definition of a deep foundation / pile

A **deep foundation** (móng sâu) transfers load to deeper, stronger strata. The **pile**
(móng cọc / cọc) is the dominant type.

### 6.1.2 Classification of piles

The handbook follows the French code **DTU 13-2**, which groups piles by how they are
made and how they transfer load:

| Group | Type |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Prefabricated piles — driven timber, steel, prestressed-concrete, driven tubular |
| 2 | Driven cast-in-place — tube driven then filled and withdrawn, or filled and left |
| 3 | Bored piles — plain, cased, slurry, auger, screw |
| 4 | Caissons (cọc giếng) |
| 5 | Pressed piles (cọc ép) — concrete and metal |
| 6 | Mini piles (cọc mini) |

**Group 1, 2 and 5** are *driven* piles: they displace and compact the soil around them,
so they mobilise **skin friction** well. But in very soft, structured soils the driving
can remould the soil and *reduce* friction. **Group 3 and 4** are *bored/replacement*
piles: the bore relieves lateral stress, so the friction they mobilise is lower for the
same nominal area.

## 6.6 Negative skin friction and lateral squeeze

### 6.6.1 The negative-skin-friction phenomenon

When the soil around a pile settles **more** than the pile, the drag reverses: the soil
now loads the pile downwards instead of supporting it. This **negative skin friction**
(ma sát âm) adds load and must be added to the applied load, not subtracted from
capacity.

## 6.11 Key takeaways

- Pile capacity is the **sum of end bearing and skin friction**: $Q_L = Q_p + Q_f$.
- **Driven** piles mobilise friction better than **bored** piles in the same soil.
- **Negative skin friction** *adds* load — never subtract it from capacity.
- Lateral capacity needs a **lateral reaction modulus**, obtainable from the Menard
  pressuremeter.
- Piles work as a **group**; account for the **group effect** and **group settlement**.
